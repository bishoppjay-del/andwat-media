QLD Christmas shutdown and storm checklist for small building and fab sites (2026)

Last reviewed 13 November 2026 · Sources linked below · Written by Jay at Andwat Co, an Australian boilermaker/fabricator (Cert III Engineering, 12+ years in the trade). About

A practical Queensland Christmas shutdown and storm-season checklist for small building and fab sites: pre-shutdown scaffold ties, planks and barricades, plant isolation and site security, post-storm re-inspection under WHS Regulation 2011 s225, and a January restart list. WorkSafe QLD and Master Builders QLD sources linked.

Queensland building and fab sites typically start their Christmas shutdown around mid-December, right in storm and cyclone season. If a storm hits a shut site, you cannot just walk back on in January and climb the scaffold. Under section 225 of the Work Health and Safety Regulation 2011 (Qld), a competent person has to inspect the scaffold and its supporting structure before use is resumed after an incident that may reasonably be expected to affect its stability. Master Builders Queensland tells sites to do a full scaffold inspection before shutdown and to have the scaffold contractor inspect it after a storm to confirm whether it is safe to use.

This guide is a practical shutdown and restart checklist for small building and fab sites: ties, planks and barricades before you lock up; plant isolation and site security; the post-storm re-inspection under s225; and a January restart list. It is general information, not legal, WHS or engineering advice, and not a certificate. It does not replace an inspection by a competent person. Confirm the current rules with WorkSafe Queensland and your scaffolding contractor.

The short answer

  • Shutdown timing: many Queensland building sites typically close from around mid-December. That window sits in what Master Builders Queensland calls traditional storm and cyclone season. There is no single legally fixed shutdown date — check your contracts and the site's own plan.
  • Before you lock up: Master Builders Queensland says the scaffolding supervisor should undertake a full pre-shutdown inspection. Check ties for strong winds, secure uppermost planks, barricade incomplete scaffold, protect foundations from stormwater, and clear loose materials off the top lift.
  • Plant and security: isolate plant you are leaving on site, and secure the site (temporary fencing, locks, signage). Prevent unauthorised access to incomplete or unattended scaffold (s225(5)).
  • After a storm: do not climb the scaffold until a competent person has inspected it, if the event may reasonably be expected to have affected its stability (s225(3)). Master Builders Queensland says to have the scaffold contractor inspect it after the storm to confirm whether it is safe to use.
  • January restart: return only when it is safe, then inspect before anyone uses the scaffold. A Christmas break does not pause the at-least-every-30-days inspection in s225(3). Record the inspection. If repairs are needed, have a competent person inspect again before use resumes (s225(4)).
  • This kit is prep only: a checklist and register, not a certificate and not a substitute for a competent person's inspection.

Why this matters at Christmas

Storm hits your shut site over Christmas: you can't climb that scaffold until it's re-inspected.

That is the practical reading of s225 together with Master Builders Queensland's severe-weather advice. WorkSafe Queensland's scaffolding page summarises the inspection duty this way: the person with management or control of a scaffold must ensure the scaffold and its supporting structure are inspected by a competent person before it is used; before use is resumed after an incident that may reasonably be expected to affect the stability of the scaffold; before use is resumed after repairs; and at least every 30 days.

Section 225 applies to suspended, cantilevered, spur and hung scaffolds, and any other scaffold from which a person or thing could fall more than 4 m. Safe Work Australia still advises inspecting scaffolds under 4 m before use and after any incident, repair, alteration or addition — so a shut small site should not ignore a smaller scaffold after a storm either. The duty sits with the person who has management or control of the scaffold at the workplace, usually the principal contractor or builder, not only the scaffolding company.

Pre-shutdown scaffold checks: ties, planks and barricades

Do this before the site goes quiet, not after the first storm warning. Master Builders Queensland's scaffolding advice for severe weather is specific. Use it as the backbone of the pre-shutdown walk:

  1. Full pre-shutdown inspection. The scaffolding supervisor should undertake a full pre-shutdown inspection of the scaffolding.
  2. Foundations. Check scaffold foundations (for example screwjacks, baseplates and sole boards) are appropriate, and ensure any potential stormwater run-off will be diverted away from the foundations. Re-tension screw-jacks where applicable.
  3. Ties. Ensure sufficient ties are in place to cater for strong winds. Ties are often the first thing other trades remove during the year; a shutdown in storm season is the wrong time to leave them short.
  4. Shadecloth and sheeting. Check the integrity of shadecloth and reattach or repair where damaged. Extra wind load on sheeting or containment needs to have been allowed for in the design.
  5. Barricades and signage. Ensure access to incomplete scaffold has been prevented through barricading and signage. That lines up with s225(5): unauthorised access to an incomplete or unattended scaffold must be prevented.
  6. Uppermost planks. Ensure all planks on the uppermost lift are secured to the transoms with tie-wire unless a captive plank system is incorporated in the design of the transoms.
  7. Loose materials. Remove all loose materials from the scaffold platforms on the uppermost lift, so they cannot become wind-blown debris.
  8. Emergency contact. Ensure an emergency contact number has been supplied by the scaffold contractor in case an emergency inspection or repairs are needed during the break — for example, after a severe storm.

If the scaffold will not be needed after the break, talk to the contractor about dismantling it before shutdown rather than leaving it standing through storm season. That is a site decision, not a legal requirement stated on the pages cited here.

Plant isolation

Master Builders Queensland's severe-weather checklist is aimed at securing the worksite (loose materials, skips, temporary fencing, drainage, delayed deliveries). It does not set out a full plant isolation procedure. For a small building or fab site, treat isolation as part of shutdown, not as an optional extra:

  • Park and isolate plant you are leaving on site. Remove keys, isolate energy sources under your site procedure, and chock or otherwise secure mobile plant so it cannot roll or be driven.
  • Do a last pre-start or shutdown check and record it. The plant pre-start checklist is the everyday version of that habit.
  • In a fab shop or on a site with welding gear, isolate power and gas, and secure cylinders. See gas cylinder storage for Australian workshops.
  • Delay material deliveries until after the break where you can, as Master Builders Queensland recommends around storms, so new packs of steel, palettes or sheet product are not left loose on an empty site.

This is practical shutdown hygiene. It is not a substitute for your SWMS, lock-out procedure, or a competent person's advice on a particular machine.

Site security

An empty site in storm season is both a weather problem and an access problem.

  • Temporary fencing. Master Builders Queensland says to check with your temporary fence supplier that the temporary fence has been installed for the potential wind loads likely during storms or cyclones, and that installation complies with regional wind categories (refer to AS 4687).
  • Clean-up. Remove loose material and rubbish, do a site clean-up and empty rubbish skips, so debris cannot blow into roads, neighbouring properties or the scaffold.
  • Drainage. Put site drainage in place to legal points of discharge to avoid flooding, including around scaffold foundations.
  • Building openings. Close windows and doors to prevent internal damage. Secure or remove loose roofing materials, fascia, gutters and flashings if they are not yet fixed down, as Master Builders Queensland describes under roofing materials.
  • Unauthorised access to scaffold. Under s225(5), the person with management or control of a scaffold must prevent unauthorised access to any incomplete or unattended scaffold. Barricades, signage and locked site perimeter all support that duty while the site is shut.
  • Emergency contact. Leave the scaffold contractor's after-hours number with whoever holds the keys, so a mid-break inspection can actually be arranged.

Post-storm re-inspection under s225

Storm hits your shut site over Christmas: you can't climb that scaffold until it's re-inspected.

WorkSafe Queensland, summarising WHS Regulation 2011 s225(3) and (4), says the person with management or control of a scaffold at a workplace must ensure that the scaffold and its supporting structure are inspected by a competent person:

  • before it is used
  • before use is resumed after an incident that may reasonably be expected to affect the stability of the scaffold
  • before use is resumed after repairs
  • at least every 30 days

If an inspection indicates that the scaffold or the supporting structure creates a risk to health and safety, any necessary repairs, alterations and additions must be carried out, and the scaffold and its supporting structure must be inspected by a competent person again before use is resumed (s225(4)).

The regulation text does not list "storms" by name. A severe storm is still the kind of incident that may reasonably be expected to affect a scaffold's stability — scour at the bases, loose or missing ties, shifted or unsecured planks, damaged shadecloth adding wind load, or impact from debris. Master Builders Queensland is direct on the weather point: after the storm, have the scaffold contractor inspect the scaffolding to confirm whether it is safe to use. Return to the worksite only when it is safe to do so, then assess or repair any damage.

Also remember s225(2): the scaffold must not be used unless the person with management or control has written confirmation from a competent person that construction of the scaffold has been completed. After alterations, repairs or additions, a new written confirmation (usually a handover certificate) should be issued and kept on site.

A daily visual check by the people who will use the scaffold is useful once work restarts, but it does not replace the competent-person inspection.

January restart list

Walk this list before anyone climbs:

  1. Return only when it is safe. Master Builders Queensland says to return to the worksite when it is safe to do so and then assess or repair any damage.
  2. Do not climb first and inspect later. If a storm or other incident may reasonably be expected to have affected stability, a competent person must inspect the scaffold and its supporting structure before use is resumed (s225(3)).
  3. Check the 30-day interval. A Christmas shutdown does not pause the "at least every 30 days" inspection. If the last competent-person inspection was more than 30 days ago, inspect before use is resumed, even if the site had quiet weather.
  4. Foundations and runoff. Look for scour, sunk sole boards, displaced baseplates and loose screw-jacks after stormwater has been through the site.
  5. Ties, planks, shadecloth and access. Confirm ties are still in place and tight, uppermost planks are still secured, shadecloth or sheeting is intact, and stairs, ladders and gates are safe.
  6. Barricades and tags. Keep incomplete or unsafe scaffold barricaded and tagged out. Prevent unauthorised access until it is cleared for use.
  7. Repairs then re-inspect. If anything needs repair, alteration or addition, have a competent person inspect again before use resumes (s225(4)), and update the written confirmation / handover.
  8. Restart plant from isolation, not from memory. Reverse isolation under your site procedure and run a pre-start before plant goes back to work.
  9. Site security and fencing. Check temporary fencing, locks and signage after wind. Confirm the fence still meets the installation you agreed with the supplier.
  10. Write it down. Record the inspection date, who inspected, the result, defects and the next due date. If a scaffold is not safe, tag it out and leave it that way until a later inspection says otherwise.

Common questions

Can I use a scaffold after a storm over the Christmas shutdown?

Not until a competent person has inspected it, if the storm is an incident that may reasonably be expected to affect the scaffold's stability. Section 225(3) of the Work Health and Safety Regulation 2011 (Qld) requires that inspection before use of the scaffold is resumed. Master Builders Queensland also says that after a storm, the scaffold contractor should inspect the scaffolding to confirm whether it is safe to use. Section 225 applies to suspended, cantilevered, spur and hung scaffolds, and any other scaffold from which a person or thing could fall more than 4 m.

What should I check on a scaffold before a Queensland Christmas shutdown?

Master Builders Queensland says the scaffolding supervisor should undertake a full pre-shutdown inspection. That includes checking foundations (screwjacks, baseplates and sole boards) and diverting potential stormwater run-off away from them, re-tensioning screw-jacks where applicable, ensuring sufficient ties are in place for strong winds, checking shadecloth, preventing access to incomplete scaffold with barricading and signage, securing planks on the uppermost lift to the transoms with tie-wire unless a captive plank system is incorporated in the design, and removing loose materials from those platforms. Keep an emergency contact number from the scaffold contractor for an inspection or repairs during the break, for example after a severe storm.

Does a Christmas shutdown pause the 30-day scaffold inspection period?

No. Section 225(3) requires a competent person to inspect the scaffold and its supporting structure at least every 30 days, as well as before the scaffold is used, before use is resumed after an incident that may reasonably be expected to affect its stability, and before use is resumed after repairs. A shutdown does not pause that interval. If the scaffold is still standing in January and more than 30 days have passed, it needs a competent-person inspection before use is resumed — and sooner if a storm or other incident may have affected its stability.

Is a checklist the same as a scaffold certificate or handover?

No. A checklist or register only records what was checked and when. It is not a certificate and it does not replace an inspection by a competent person, or the written confirmation section 225(2) requires before a scaffold is used.
