Plant pre-start checklist template: what to check every shift

A practical plant pre-start checklist template for Australian sites: walk-around items for forklifts, EWPs, excavators and loaders, a defect tag-out snippet, and where to keep the records.

Most hire companies and civil sites will not let you start a forklift, EWP or excavator until a daily pre-start is filled in. Manufacturer checklists vary; the WHS duty is to make sure plant is safe to use. This page gives you a template structure you can print and adapt.

The short answer

  • Do a pre-start at the start of each shift, for each machine, before it moves.
  • Record plant ID, hour meter, operator, ticket number, date and shift.
  • Walk-around first (tyres/tracks, leaks, pins, guards, lights, horn, reverse alarm), then function checks (brakes, steering, controls, e-stop).
  • Faults go in a defect register with tag-out. Do not "run it careful" until it is fixed or a competent person clears it.
  • Keep the sheets – insurers and investigators ask for them after incidents.

Printable pre-start snippet (tick OK / FAULT / N/A)

  • Walk-around: body damage, loose guards, missing pins
  • Tyres or tracks: pressure, cuts, tension
  • Fluids: engine oil, hydraulic oil, coolant, fuel – no puddles under the machine
  • Hydraulic hoses and rams: leaks, bulges, damaged fittings
  • Lights, horn, reverse / travel alarm
  • Seatbelt / restraint and operator seat condition
  • Brakes, steering, controls, attachments
  • ROPS / FOPS condition (where fitted)
  • Fire extinguisher present and in date
  • Load chart readable; e-stop works
  • Safe to operate? Yes / No – signature

Worked example – site forklift, Monday morning

Plant ID FLT-04 · hours 4,812 · operator Mia (LF ticket). Walk-around finds a weeping hydraulic fitting on the tilt ram → FAULT, red tag, entered in defect register, machine quarantined. Spare FLT-02 pre-started clean and used instead. Fitting replaced same day; FLT-04 cleared and returned to hire pool.

Quick answers

Is a pre-start legally required every day?

WHS laws require plant to be maintained and inspected so it stays safe. Daily pre-starts are the common way sites meet that duty and hire-company rules. Follow the manufacturer and your site procedure.

What if I find a fault mid-shift?

Stop, tag out, record it, and do not use the machine until a competent person clears it.

Can one checklist cover every machine type?

Use a common core (walk-around, fluids, brakes, e-stop) plus machine-specific items from the manufacturer for EWPs, excavators and cranes.

Where should records be kept?

On site for the current job, then filed by plant ID. A register of next-inspection due dates stops overdue gear staying on the floor.
