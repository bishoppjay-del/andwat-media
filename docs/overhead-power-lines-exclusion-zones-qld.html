Overhead power lines in QLD: exclusion zones under the Electrical Safety Regulation 2026

Last reviewed 28 November 2026 · Sources linked below · Written by Jay at Andwat Co, an Australian boilermaker/fabricator (Cert III Engineering, 12+ years in the trade). About

How close tradies, cranes, EWPs and trucks can get to overhead power lines in Queensland under Schedule 2 of the Electrical Safety Regulation 2026, what counts toward the distance, spotters and what to do if you can't keep clear.

A crane truck dropping steel at the gate, an EWP going up to fix a gutter bracket, a 6 m length of RHS carried on a shoulder, an aluminium extension ladder walked upright across a yard. Every one of those can reach a live overhead line. In Queensland the Electrical Safety Regulation 2026 replaced the Electrical Safety Regulation 2013 on 1 September 2026, and the exclusion-zone distances now sit in its Schedule 2. Here's what they are for ordinary workers, plant and vehicles, and what has to happen if you can't keep out of them.

The short answer

  • The duty: a business at a workplace must ensure, so far as is reasonably practicable, that no person, plant or thing comes within an unsafe distance of an overhead electric line (Electrical Safety Regulation 2026, s 13(1), maximum penalty 60 penalty units).
  • Unsafe distance = inside the exclusion zone in Schedule 2 for that person, plant or vehicle (ss 10 and 11).
  • It counts what you're holding. A person includes clothing and any conductive object they're handling. Plant includes the load it's handling. A vehicle includes whatever it's carrying (s 11(3)). The length of steel and the crane's load count.
  • Most tradies are "untrained persons". Unless you're authorised by the person in control of the line (or working under someone who is), the bigger distances apply: 3 m for a person near an uninsulated low-voltage line unless the person in control of the line has been consulted, and 3 m for operating plant either way.
  • Can't stay out? Then a risk assessment is required and the controls must match it, plus any requirements of the electricity entity responsible for the line (s 13(2)).

Exclusion zones for untrained persons (overhead uninsulated lines)

From Schedule 2, part 2, division 1 of the Electrical Safety Regulation 2026. Distances are in millimetres, measured from the line, and WorkSafe Queensland's code of practice says exclusion zones extend in all directions, not just sideways.

Schedule 2 also has tables for DC lines, for insulated lines, and smaller distances for authorised and instructed persons. Two catches: insulated-line distances only help if the insulation has been checked (the code says insulation must be inspected by a competent person, and if it hasn't been found safe, treat the line as uninsulated), and plant run by an authorised or instructed person without a safety observer or another safe system is treated as run by an untrained person (Schedule 2, s 2). If you don't know the voltage, don't guess: ask the electricity entity.

Who counts as "authorised" or "instructed"

An authorised person has enough technical knowledge and experience for work near the line and has been approved by the person in control of the line (or is authorised to act for them). An instructed person is working under the supervision of an authorised person. Everyone else is an untrained person for that line (Schedule 2, s 1). A trade ticket, a white card or an EWP licence doesn't, on its own, make you authorised for a particular line: the approval comes from the person in control of that line.

Controls, best first

WorkSafe Queensland's code of practice for working near overhead and underground electric lines lists the hierarchy:

  • Eliminate: have the line de-energised (isolated and earthed), re-routed, or put underground. Arrange this with the electricity entity early, because it can take time.
  • Substitute: use plant that physically can't reach the exclusion zone, non-conductive tools, or ultrasonic height measuring instead of a measuring stick.
  • Isolate: a physical barrier of non-conductive material such as wood or plastic.
  • Engineering: mechanical stops, programmable zone-limiting devices, slew limits, insulated plant.
  • Administrative: proximity alarms, warning signs, visual indicators (such as tiger tails) fitted to low-voltage conductors by the electricity entity and checked each day by a competent person, a SWMS where required, an emergency and rescue plan for line contact, and a safety observer.
  • PPE last: electrically tested insulating gloves, rubber-soled boots, helmets.

The safety observer (spotter)

The code says a crane or plant is in the "safety observer zone" when any part of it, a person in an EWP, a hand-held tool or the load could enter the exclusion zone during the lift. In that zone a safety observer should be used. The observer's only job is watching that one piece of plant and warning the operator, and they need to be able to communicate with the operator effectively. You're not in the safety observer zone if high-voltage lines are de-energised and earthed, or limiting devices or physical barriers stop the plant and load reaching the exclusion zone.

SWMS: this is high risk construction work

Construction work carried out on or near energised electrical installations or services is high risk construction work under the QLD Work Health and Safety Regulation 2011 (s 291(k)), so a SWMS is needed before it starts. See the SWMS for high risk construction work guide.

A 60-second pre-lift check for small crews

  • Look up before you park, slew, raise a tipper, boom up or stand a ladder up. Note every line over or near the work area, including service lines to the building.
  • Assume you're an untrained person. Use the "without consultation" distances unless the person in control of the line has actually been consulted.
  • Count the longest thing: the jib at full reach, the load swinging, the steel on your shoulder, the ladder as you walk it.
  • If any of it could get inside the zone: stop, do the risk assessment, contact the electricity entity, and put a safety observer on.
  • Brief the crew on what to do if plant touches a line, as part of the emergency plan.

The code still quotes the 2013 regulation

WorkSafe Queensland's codes page lists the overhead and underground lines code as commencing 2 January 2020 and varied on 1 January 2025 (codes of practice). Its text refers to the Electrical Safety Regulation 2013, so section numbers in the code (such as s 68 and s 69) don't match the 2026 Regulation (s 13 and s 11). WorkSafe Queensland confirms the 2026 Regulation replaced the 2013 Regulation on 1 September 2026 (WorkSafe Queensland). Check the current Regulation for the legal wording.

FAQ

How close can you work to power lines in Queensland?

It depends on the line voltage, whether it's insulated, and who you are. Under Schedule 2 of the Electrical Safety Regulation 2026, an untrained person must stay 3,000 mm from an uninsulated low-voltage line unless the person in control of the line has been consulted (then 1,000 mm), and operating plant run by an untrained person must stay 3,000 mm from lines up to 132 kV.

What replaced the Electrical Safety Regulation 2013 in Queensland?

The Electrical Safety Regulation 2026, which commenced on 1 September 2026. The overhead line exclusion zones are now in its Schedule 2, the meaning of unsafe distance is in s 11 and the duty to keep people, plant and things out is in s 13.

Does the steel I'm carrying count in the exclusion zone?

Yes. Under s 11(3) a person includes any article of clothing and any conductive object the person is handling, operating plant includes anything it's handling, and a vehicle includes anything it's carrying. Only insulated extensions that have been tested and found safe for use near the line are excluded.

Do I need a spotter when using a crane near power lines?

WorkSafe Queensland's code of practice says a safety observer should be used when a crane or operating plant is in the safety observer zone, meaning any part of it, the load or a person in an EWP could enter the exclusion zone. Under Schedule 2, plant run by an authorised person without a safety observer or another safe system is treated as run by an untrained person.
