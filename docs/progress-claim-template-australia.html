Progress claim template Australia: what to include, a free column layout, and the Queensland payment claim rules

Last reviewed 26 November 2026 · Sources linked below · Written by Jay at Andwat Co, an Australian boilermaker/fabricator (Cert III Engineering, 12+ years in the trade). About

A progress claim is a staged request for payment for work done so far on a construction job. Get the format right and it's a payment claim you can enforce; get it wrong and you can lose your fast-track dispute rights. Below is a free layout that works for any trade, and the Queensland rules it has to meet.

The short answer

  • Progress claim vs invoice: a progress claim asks to be paid for the value of work completed to date under a construction contract, less what's already been paid. An invoice usually bills a finished job or a fixed amount. You can use your tax invoice as the claim, as long as it also meets the payment claim rules.
  • In Queensland the Building Industry Fairness (Security of Payment) Act 2017 (BIF Act) says a payment claim must identify the work (or related goods and services) it covers, state the amount claimed and request payment of it. QBCC says there's no prescribed format, and the requirements apply even if you use your usual tax invoice (QBCC – Request payment).
  • Send it on or after a reference date. That's the date in your contract, or, if there isn't one, the last day of the month the work was done. Only one claim per reference date.
  • Payment is due on the contract's due date or, if none, 10 business days after the claim is given. The QBCC Act caps contract payment terms at 25 business days for subcontracts and 15 business days for commercial building contracts.
  • The respondent must reply by paying in full or giving a payment schedule within 15 business days (or sooner if the contract says so), with reasons for paying less (QBCC security of payment guide).

Free progress claim template: the header

  • Your business name, ABN, licence number (if licensed) and contact details
  • Respondent's name (the party who has to pay you), project name and site address
  • Contract or purchase order number and the contract sum
  • Claim number, the reference date it's made on, the date issued, and the period it covers
  • The words "This is a payment claim" plus a sentence requesting payment of the claimed amount by the due date
  • Tax invoice details if you're GST-registered: "Tax invoice", GST amount and the buyer's identity or ABN

Free progress claim template: the line items

ColumnWhat goes in it
Item / stageContract line, schedule of rates item or stage name (e.g. "Level 1 rough-in").
Scheduled valueThe contract value of that item.
% complete to dateYour honest progress, backed by photos, a site diary or delivery dockets.
Value completed to dateScheduled value × % complete.
Previously claimedWhat you claimed for this line in earlier claims.
This claimValue to date minus previously claimed.
Approved variationsSeparate lines, each with its variation number and approval reference.
Pending variationsList them separately and mark them as unapproved, so the approved total stays clean.

Then the summary: gross value to date (contract work plus approved variations), less previous claims, equals this claim; less retention withheld if the contract allows it; plus GST if you're registered (check the GST treatment of retention with your BAS agent); equals the amount claimed. Attach the evidence: progress photos, the variation approvals, and delivery dockets for materials on site.

Worked example (made-up numbers): a $120,000 subcontract. At claim 3 you're 60% through ($72,000), plus a $4,000 approved variation, so $76,000 gross to date. Previous claims total $48,000, so this claim is $28,000. The contract allows 10% cash retention on each payment, so $2,800 is withheld and $25,200 is payable now, before GST. If you're GST-registered, add GST as your contract and tax invoice require; how GST applies to retention is a question for your BAS agent. Check the retention against the 5% overall cap in the retention guide.

Time limits in Queensland

QBCC says a progress claim (not a final claim) must be given before the later of the date in the contract or six months after the work was completed. A final payment claim has until the later of the contract date, six months after completion, or 28 days after the end of the last defects liability period (QBCC). The BIF Act payment claim guide walks through adjudication if you're short-paid.

Home building jobs (QLD domestic contracts)

On a Queensland domestic building contract over $3,300, Schedule 1B of the QBCC Act says the builder can't claim an amount (other than the deposit) unless it relates directly to the progress of the work on site and is no more than the value of that work. The contract's stages should reflect that (QBCC Act Sch 1B s 34). See the QBCC $3,300 rule and deposits guide.

Other states

Every state and territory has its own security of payment law, with different time limits and wording requirements. Use the same layout, but check your state's Act or regulator before you rely on the claim.

FAQ

What is a progress claim in construction, and how is it different from an invoice?

A progress claim asks to be paid for the value of work completed to date under a construction contract, less amounts already paid. An invoice normally bills a finished job or fixed amount. In Queensland your tax invoice can be your payment claim, as long as it identifies the work, states the amount claimed and requests payment, as the BIF Act requires.

What must a payment claim include in Queensland?

Under the BIF Act a payment claim must identify the construction work or related goods and services it relates to, state the amount claimed, and request payment of that amount. QBCC says there's no prescribed format. Some head contractors also have to include a supporting statement when claiming from a principal.

When can I submit a progress claim?

On or after each reference date: the date set in your contract or, if the contract doesn't say, the last day of the month in which the work was done. Only one payment claim can be given for each reference date, and a claim sent early may be invalid.

How long does the other party have to respond to a progress claim in Queensland?

They must pay in full by the due date or give a payment schedule within 15 business days after receiving the claim, or a shorter time if the contract says so. The payment schedule must state what they'll pay and their reasons for paying less.
