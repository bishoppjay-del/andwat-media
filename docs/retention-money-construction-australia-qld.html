Retention money in construction: the Queensland caps, when you get it back, and where retention trusts stand

Last reviewed 26 November 2026 · Sources linked below · Written by Jay at Andwat Co, an Australian boilermaker/fabricator (Cert III Engineering, 12+ years in the trade). About

Retention is money held back from your progress payments as security for defects. In Queensland the QBCC Act caps how much can be held and when it has to be released, and it's an offence to sit on it without a reasonable excuse. Here's how much can be held, when you should get it back, and where the retention trust rules stand as of November 2026.

The short answer (Queensland commercial work and subcontracts)

  • Per payment: no more than 10% of each progress payment can be withheld as retention (QBCC Act s 67M).
  • Before practical completion: total retention plus securities held can't exceed 5% of the contract price (s 67L for subcontracts, s 67K for principal contracts). Retention held to protect payments for things not yet installed is outside the cap.
  • After practical completion: it drops to 2.5% of the contract price, held only for defects identified in the defects liability period (s 67N).
  • No release date in the contract? It must be released 12 months after practical completion (s 67NA).
  • Failing to release it on time without a reasonable excuse is an offence, with a maximum of 200 penalty units or 1 year's imprisonment (s 67NB).

These rules are in Part 4A of the QBCC Act 1991, which covers building contracts other than domestic building contracts. QBCC summarises them in its contractor payments guide and on its retentions and securities page.

Example (made-up numbers): a $200,000 subcontract. The 5% cap is $10,000 before practical completion. If the contract takes 10% from each claim, retention stops building up once $10,000 is held. After practical completion the cap is $5,000 (2.5%), so $5,000 should come back to you then, unless the contract releases it on different terms within the caps.

One exception: for a principal's head contract (not a subcontract), the 5% condition can be excluded if the contract is in writing, explains the condition, expressly says it doesn't apply, and that clause is initialled by both parties (s 67K(4)). Subcontracts don't have that opt-out.

Getting it back: the notices

  • End of defects liability period notice (s 67NC): a contractor holding your retention must give you the approved form (QBCC's S67NC notice) within 10 business days before the defects liability period ends, stating the end date, the amount to be paid if no defects need fixing, and the proposed payment date. If the period is linked to a head contract, it's 5 business days after they get their own notice. This doesn't apply to a principal.
  • Your final payment claim: QBCC says most contracts need a final payment claim to release retention. It must be given before the later of the contract date, six months after completion, or 28 days after the end of the last defects liability period (QBCC – Request payment).
  • Using your retention for defects (s 67J): before the contracting party uses retention or a security to recover money you owe, it must give you written notice of the proposed use and the amount, within 28 days of becoming aware (or when it ought to have become aware) of its right to that money.

Retention trust accounts: the status right now

The BIF Act requires a retention trust when cash retention is withheld under a "withholding contract", which is essentially a contract on a project trust project, subject to exemptions (for example, where the party withholding is the State, the Commonwealth or a local government) (BIF Act Ch 2 Pt 3).

On 10 February 2025 the Queensland Government paused the further rollout of trust accounts to private projects under $10 million. QBCC says the planned phases for contracts of $3 million (1 March 2025) and $1 million (1 October 2025) will not occur (QBCC, QBCC Building Reg Reno). As at the Department of Housing and Public Works' update of 17 July 2026, the framework still applies to:

  • eligible Queensland Government contracts of $1 million or more
  • private sector, local government, statutory authority and government-owned corporation contracts of $10 million or more

Tranche 4 of the reforms proposes "further reducing administrative burden associated with trust accounts", but no detail or start date had been published at our review date (Building Reg Reno). So for most small subbies on private jobs under $10 million, retention isn't held in a trust account. The Part 4A caps and release rules above still apply. Use QBCC's online trust account tool if you're not sure about a particular job.

Home building (domestic) contracts

Part 4A doesn't apply to domestic building contracts. Home jobs in Queensland are governed by Schedule 1B instead (deposits, progress payments tied to the work, variations). See the $3,300 rule and deposits guide.

Other states

Retention caps, trust requirements and release rules differ across the states. Use your state regulator's guidance, and read the retention, practical completion and defects clauses before you sign. The steel fabricator guide has a practical checklist for subcontract retention clauses.

Track it so it doesn't get forgotten

Retention gets lost because the release date is 12 months or more after you've moved on. For each job, write down the contract price, the retention % per claim, the cap, the practical completion date, the defects liability period end date, and the date you sent your final payment claim. Put the release date in your calendar the day practical completion is reached.

FAQ

How much retention can be held on a construction contract in Queensland?

Under Part 4A of the QBCC Act, no more than 10% of each progress payment can be withheld, and total retention plus securities can't exceed 5% of the contract price before practical completion, falling to 2.5% after practical completion. A principal's head contract can opt out of the 5% condition only in a written, explained clause initialled by both parties; subcontracts can't.

When do you get retention money back?

When your contract says, which is usually at practical completion for part and at the end of the defects liability period for the rest. If the contract has no identifiable release period, the QBCC Act says it must be released 12 months after practical completion. Most contracts also need you to submit a final payment claim.

Do Queensland subcontractors' retentions have to be held in a trust account?

Only on project trust projects. As at November 2026 that means eligible Queensland Government contracts of $1 million or more, and private sector and other contracts of $10 million or more. The planned rollout to smaller private projects was paused in February 2025 and QBCC says those lower thresholds will not occur.

Can the head contractor use my retention to fix defects?

They can only use retention or a security to recover an amount you owe if they give you written notice of the proposed use and the amount within 28 days of becoming aware of their right to it (QBCC Act s 67J).
