Subcontractor vs employee for tradies: ATO 6 checks, super & WorkCover QLD

Last reviewed 26 November 2026 · Sources linked below · Written by Jay at Andwat Co, an Australian boilermaker/fabricator (Cert III Engineering, 12+ years in the trade). About

Simply getting an invoice with an ABN on it does not make someone an independent contractor. If the contract and working arrangement look like employment — or if you pay a sole-trader subbie mainly for their personal labour — you can still owe super, PAYG withholding and WorkCover QLD.

Why an ABN and a contract label aren't enough

The ATO is clear: calling someone an "independent contractor" in a written agreement does not decide their status. Following the High Court decisions in CFMMEU v Personnel Contracting and ZG Operations v Jamsek, the ATO looks at the totality of the legal rights and obligations in the contract you entered into (written, oral or varied by conduct) to answer one core question: is the worker serving in your business, or operating their own independent business?

The ATO also states that apprentices, trainees and labourers working under your direction are always employees for tax and super purposes.

The 6 factors the ATO and WorkCover QLD check

No single factor decides the answer on its own, and you shouldn't treat them as a tick-a-box score, but the ATO and WorkCover Queensland both focus on these six areas:

FactorPoints toward EmployeePoints toward Independent Contractor
1. Control over workYou have the legal right to direct how, where and when the work is done (e.g. set shift hours).The subbie decides how, where and when they do the work, subject to agreed specs/deadlines.
2. IntegrationWorks within and represents your business (e.g. your uniform, your branding, ongoing roster).Operates their own business independently and is free to accept or decline other jobs.
3. Basis of paymentPaid by the hour/shift, per item, or on commission.Paid to achieve a specific result (usually a fixed quote) on a tax invoice when completed.
4. Delegation / subcontractingMust do the work personally and cannot pay someone else to do it.Has a real contractual right to delegate or subcontract the work to their own crew and pays them.
5. Tools and equipmentYou supply the tools/plant, or pay an allowance/reimbursement for theirs.Supplies and pays for their own tools, ute, equipment and consumables.
6. Commercial riskYour business wears the cost of fixing mistakes or defects.Legally responsible for fixing their own defective work at their own cost.

The super guarantee trap for "labour-only" sole-trader subbies

Even where a worker is genuinely an independent contractor for tax, subsection 12(3) of the Superannuation Guarantee (Administration) Act treats an individual contractor as an employee for super if you pay them under a contract that is wholly or principally for their personal labour and skills (more than half the dollar value is labour), they must perform the work personally (cannot delegate), and payment isn't dependent on achieving a specified result.

  • Rate and 1 July 2026 rules: From 1 July 2026, you must pay at least the 12% super guarantee rate on the contractor's qualifying earnings (all payments relating to their labour, including overtime), paid into their super fund on time. You cannot just add 12% cash onto their invoice.
  • Materials & GST excluded: Payments for materials, plant hire or GST are excluded. If an invoice bundles labour and materials, separate them or apportion using reasonable market value and keep records showing how you calculated it.
  • Companies, trusts & partnerships: If your contract is directly with a company, trust or partnership (rather than an individual sole trader), you do not pay super guarantee for the person they employ to do the work.

WorkCover Queensland: who you must cover

Under Queensland's Workers' Compensation and Rehabilitation Act 2003, only an individual (including a sole trader) can be a "worker" — not a corporation, partnership or trust. WorkCover QLD applies a four-step test (contract for performance of work, specific exclusions, specific inclusions such as labour hire, and the contract-of-service test using the ATO's 6 common law principles). If a sole-trader subbie is deemed a worker, you must declare their payments on your WorkCover policy.

Before any genuine subbie steps onto site, verify their entity type on ABN Lookup and check their Certificate of Currency (public liability and WorkCover/personal accident), their licences and HRWL expiry dates, and log reportable payments for your 28 August TPAR.

Common questions

If a subbie has an ABN and sends tax invoices, do I ever owe them super?

Yes. Under ATO rules, if you contract directly with an individual sole trader under an arrangement wholly or principally (more than 50%) for their personal labour and skills, they must do the work themselves, and they aren't paid for a specified result, you must pay 12% super guarantee into their super fund on the labour component.

Can I just pay a sole-trader subbie 12% extra cash instead of paying a super fund?

No. The ATO specifically states that paying an extra amount equal to the SG percentage directly to the contractor on top of their pay does not count as a super contribution — you must pay it into their complying super fund by the due date to avoid the Super Guarantee Charge.

Do I need WorkCover QLD for a subcontractor who operates as a Pty Ltd company?

Under Queensland's Workers' Compensation and Rehabilitation Act 2003, only an individual (including a sole trader) can be a worker, not a corporation, partnership or trust. Still check their Certificate of Currency to confirm the entity name matches your contract and they insure their own workers.
