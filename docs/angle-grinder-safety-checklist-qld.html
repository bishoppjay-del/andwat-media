Angle grinder safety checklist (QLD): guards, discs, RPM and PPE

Last reviewed 27 November 2026 · Sources linked below · Written by Jay at Andwat Co, an Australian boilermaker/fabricator (Cert III Engineering, 12+ years in the trade). About

A bench-side angle grinder safety checklist from WorkSafe Queensland alerts: guard on, disc RPM rating, disc size and hole, flange and nut, 9-inch grinder risk assessment, PPE and exclusion zones.

Every fab shop has one: the 9-inch grinder with the guard "taken off so it fits", a cutting disc being used to grind, and an old disc with a chip out of it. WorkSafe Queensland has put out several alerts on angle grinders after deaths and serious injuries, including a worker killed in Brisbane in June 2021 while cutting a structural steel member with an unguarded 9-inch (230 mm) grinder. Here's the checklist version of what WorkSafe Queensland says to do, in the order you'd actually check it on the bench.

The short answer

  • Guard on, always. WorkSafe Queensland says guards should be used on all angle grinders, using the correct guard supplied by the manufacturer (2021 incident alert).
  • Disc rated speed ≥ grinder rated speed. Both the disc and the grinder must be marked with their maximum operating speed in RPM, and the disc's rating must not be lower than the grinder's.
  • Right size, right hole. Never fit a bigger disc than the grinder is made for. The 2016 alert describes a 14-inch (356 mm) cut-off disc on a 9-inch (230 mm) grinder pushing edge speed from about 70 m/s to at least 120 m/s (guards and discs alert).
  • Grinding disc for grinding. Grinding with a cutting disc damages it and makes it more likely to break.
  • Two hands, run up to speed, exclusion zone. Hold it with both hands using the side handle, let it reach full speed before it touches the job, and keep other people out of the line of fire.
  • Think twice about 9-inch. WorkSafe Queensland says a risk assessment to find alternative methods and tools should be done before selecting a 9-inch (230 mm) grinder, and notes some sites have banned them.

Before you plug it in

CheckWhat "pass" looks like
Right tool?Could a bandsaw, cold saw, drop saw or a smaller grinder do it instead? For 9-inch grinders WorkSafe Queensland expects this question answered in a risk assessment first.
GuardThe manufacturer's guard is fitted, the right way round, and not cut down.
Disc size and holeDisc diameter matches the grinder. The centre hole fits the spindle flange with no slop. An oversize hole can run off-centre, vibrate and shatter.
Speed ratingDisc RPM rating marked and not less than the grinder's RPM rating.
Disc conditionNo cracks, chips or damage. Damaged discs go in the bin, not back on the shelf.
Flange and nutThe flange and lock nut supplied by the manufacturer, fitted the way the manual shows. Cutting and grinding discs can need a different fitting. Tighten with the manufacturer's tool, not a punch and hammer.
Lead and powerLead in good condition with a current test tag, and an RCD (safety switch) in the circuit. Both are on WorkSafe Queensland's angle grinder self-assessment tool.
Multi-cutters and saw bladesDon't use them unless the grinder has full spring-loaded guarding and the accessory is suitable for that tool (multi-cutters alert).

PPE WorkSafe Queensland lists

Safety glasses ("medium impact" as a minimum on the self-assessment tool), a face shield if required, hearing protection, a dust mask suited to the task, gloves, a leather apron or jacket against sparks, and safety footwear. No loose clothing or jewellery, and long hair tied back. PPE is the last line of defence: the guard, the right disc and an exclusion zone come first.

While you're cutting or grinding

  • Clamp or support the job. Don't hold small pieces in your other hand.
  • Sparks away from you, other workers and anything flammable. If you're near combustibles, that's hot work: see the hot work permit guide.
  • Disc not touching the work when you switch on. Work at about waist height, not reaching up and away.
  • Don't lock the trigger on, and don't carry it running at your side.
  • Lead behind you. No liquid coolants on electric grinders (shock risk).
  • Unplug before changing or cleaning discs. Wait for the disc to stop before you put it down.

What to keep on file

If a worker is hurt, WorkSafe Queensland says you'll need to show the regulator you used an effective risk management process. For a small shop, that usually means: a toolbox talk on grinders with attendance signed, the grinder in your plant register with its test-and-tag date, and a defect log showing damaged gear was tagged out. The silica angle matters too if you cut or grind concrete, brick or stone: see the silica risk control plan guide.

FAQ

Is it illegal to remove the guard from an angle grinder in Queensland?

WorkSafe Queensland's guidance says guards should be used on all angle grinders and that you must ensure the correct guard supplied by the manufacturer is fitted before operating larger grinders. Running a grinder with the guard removed makes it much harder to show you've managed the risk as the Work Health and Safety Act 2011 requires.

Can I put a bigger disc on my angle grinder?

No. WorkSafe Queensland's alert describes a 14-inch (356 mm) cut-off disc fitted to a 9-inch (230 mm) grinder pushing the disc's edge speed from about 70 m/s to at least 120 m/s, risking the disc shattering. The disc's rated RPM must not be lower than the grinder's rated RPM, and the centre hole must match the spindle flange.

Are 9-inch angle grinders banned in Queensland?

Not across the board. WorkSafe Queensland notes 9-inch (230 mm) grinders have been banned on some work sites and says a risk assessment to identify alternative methods and tools should be carried out before selecting one. Check the principal contractor's site rules.

What PPE do I need for an angle grinder?

WorkSafe Queensland lists safety glasses (medium impact as a minimum on its self-assessment tool), a face shield if required, hearing protection, a dust mask suited to the task, gloves, a leather apron or jacket, and safety footwear. No loose clothing or jewellery, and tie back long hair.
