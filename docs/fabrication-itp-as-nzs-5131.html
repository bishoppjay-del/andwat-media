What goes in a fabrication ITP (AS/NZS 5131 projects)

Last reviewed 25 October 2026 · Sources linked below · Written by Jay at Andwat Co, an Australian boilermaker/fabricator (Cert III Engineering, 12+ years in the trade). About

Plain-English guide to inspection and test plans for structural steel fabrication on AS/NZS 5131 jobs: hold and witness points, typical activities and the records builders ask for.

On structural steel jobs specified to AS/NZS 5131 (Structural steelwork – Fabrication and erection), the builder or engineer will usually ask the fabricator for an Inspection and Test Plan (ITP) and a set of quality records before they accept the steel. This page explains the idea in plain English. It doesn't reproduce the standard, so buy AS/NZS 5131 and read the job specification for the actual requirements.

What an ITP is

An ITP is a table of every inspection or test activity on the job, in order. For each activity it shows what is checked, against which document or criteria, who checks it, what record is produced, and what kind of point it is:

  • Hold point (H): work stops until the nominated person checks and releases it.
  • Witness point (W): the client or engineer is notified and can attend, but work can go on if they don't.
  • Review (R) or surveillance (S): records are reviewed, or there are spot checks.

Activities that typically appear

The Australian Steel Institute's standard steelwork drawing notes list an ITP as required for each of these items:

  • Materials and components
  • Preparation and assembly
  • Welding
  • Mechanical fastening
  • Surface treatment
  • Paint coatings
  • Galvanized coatings
  • Erection

Construction category drives the effort

AS/NZS 5131 uses construction categories CC1 to CC4. The designer works them out from the importance level, service category and fabrication category, and they can differ for different parts of a structure. Higher categories mean more inspection. Check the drawings or specification for the CC before you build the ITP.

Records builders commonly ask for

ASI's steelwork compliance management plan template lists checks and records such as:

  • Mill test certificates, reviewed when material is delivered
  • Approval of the welding procedure specification (WPS), and welder qualifications
  • Fit-up checks and visual weld inspection, plus NDT where specified
  • Coating checks, including dry film thickness (DFT) measurements
  • Non-conformance reports (NCRs) and how each was closed out
  • Pre-delivery inspection

Want more detail?

The ASI publishes a guidance note on building an ITP that satisfies AS/NZS 5131 §13. It's sold through its book shop and is free to member companies.
