How to chase an unpaid invoice in Australia (tradies and sole traders)

Last reviewed 8 November 2026 · Sources linked below · Written by Jay at Andwat Co, an Australian boilermaker/fabricator (Cert III Engineering, 12+ years in the trade). About

Step-by-step for Australian tradies: check the agreement, send reminders, write a letter of demand, use ASBFEO dispute support, file a QCAT minor debt claim up to $25,000, or claim a GST bad debt adjustment. Official sources linked.

Most unpaid invoices get paid after a polite nudge. The ones that don't need a calm, documented escalation: reminder, second reminder, letter of demand, then dispute help or a tribunal claim. This guide follows the steps on business.gov.au and the Australian Small Business and Family Enterprise Ombudsman (ASBFEO), with the Queensland QCAT minor debt rules for claims up to $25,000. It is general information for Australian tradies and sole traders, not legal or tax advice.

The short answer

  • Check the agreement — the due date, payment method and anything agreed about late payment (written contract, or emails/texts for a verbal deal).
  • Remind, then remind again — phone, email or text on the day it goes overdue, then a firmer second reminder and a final notice.
  • Letter of demand — amount, what it's for, a firm pay-by date, copies of the invoice and reminders, sent by registered post with signed proof of delivery.
  • Get help — ASBFEO's free Dispute Support tool and mediation, or a debt collection service.
  • QLD tribunal — QCAT minor debt claim for fixed or agreed amounts up to $25,000 (excluding interest), agreement no more than 6 years old.
  • Construction payment claims in QLD work differently — see the BIF Act payment claim guide for the statutory route (payment schedules, suspension, adjudication).

Step 1: Check what you agreed

business.gov.au's first step is to read the contract. Confirm the date the customer should have paid, the payment method, and what you agreed would happen if payment was late. If the deal was verbal or part verbal, gather proof of what was agreed — the quote they accepted, emails, texts and photos of the finished work. Also check you invoiced the right legal entity: the person you dealt with may not own the business. Search the business name or ABN on the ABN Lookup before you escalate.

If you're GST-registered, make sure the invoice is a valid tax invoice (see what a tax invoice must show). If you're not registered, don't charge GST — see sole trader invoices when you're not GST-registered. A wrong invoice gives a slow payer an easy excuse.

Step 2: Friendly reminder, second reminder, final notice

A missed payment could just be an oversight. Get in touch by phone, email, text or letter to give a friendly reminder, discuss a new payment date, or offer a payment plan. A simple rhythm many small trade businesses use:

  1. Day 1 overdue — friendly reminder with the invoice re-attached and your bank details.
  2. Day 7–14 — second reminder: "this invoice is now X days overdue, please pay by [date] or call me to arrange a plan."
  3. Day 21–28 — final notice: the next step will be a formal letter of demand.

Log every contact (date, how, what was said). That log becomes evidence later. Business Victoria publishes free reminder, second reminder and final notice email templates — business.gov.au links to them.

Step 3: Write a letter of demand

A letter of demand puts the debt in writing and gives the customer a last chance before further action. Per business.gov.au it should:

  • Have "Letter of demand" as the heading
  • State exactly how much is owed, what for, and the date it must be paid by
  • Only mention action you're actually prepared to take (for example, lodging a QCAT claim)
  • Only include a late-payment interest rate if it was specified in the contract
  • Be accurate, polite and respectful — nothing false or misleading
  • Be signed and dated, with copies of the contract or quote, invoice, reminders and relevant emails attached

Send it by registered post with signed proof of delivery and keep the card. A lawyer can write one on letterhead for a set fee if you'd rather. Remember debt collection rules apply to you as a creditor too: the ACCC and ASIC debt collection guideline bans harassment, coercion and misleading threats — no repeated late-night calls, no turning up at a customer's home to pressure them, no threats of action you can't take.

Step 4: Dispute help, mediation or a debt collector

If the letter of demand doesn't work, ASBFEO's free Dispute Support online tool points you to the right low-cost resolution service (it may be ASBFEO itself or a state service), and ASBFEO can arrange alternative dispute resolution such as mediation. It's generally quicker and cheaper than court. Phone 1300 650 460 if it's urgent.

A debt collection service is the other option. business.gov.au notes most commission rates sit between 5% and 30% of the debt. Tell the customer you plan to use one — that alone sometimes gets you paid. It can strain the relationship, so weigh it against future work from that client.

Step 5: QCAT minor debt claim (Queensland, up to $25,000)

In Queensland, QCAT hears minor debt disputes — claims for a fixed or agreed amount of money (a liquidated demand) from a person, business or company. The application must:

  • be for no more than $25,000 (excluding interest)
  • involve a previous agreement about payment — QCAT lists an unpaid invoice, account or demand, and work done or goods supplied at a cost agreed beforehand
  • relate to an agreement made no more than 6 years ago

You need the respondent's full name and correct address and copies of the agreement and invoice. You pick the QCAT location where it will be heard, pay the filing fee (it's set by your claim amount — check QCAT's current fee), and lodge through the QCase online portal, in person or by mail. You can ask to add the filing fee and ASIC search fees to your claim, and claim interest at an agreed rate or as decided by the tribunal. You must serve a copy of the application on the respondent, following QCAT's service rules. Over $25,000, you can reduce the claim to $25,000 or start in the Magistrates Court (up to $150,000) — get legal advice. Other states have their own tribunals or local courts for small claims.

Step 6: Writing it off — GST and tax

Chasing costs time. business.gov.au notes sometimes the best option is to stop chasing and write off the debt. If you're GST-registered on a non-cash (accruals) basis and already paid GST on that sale, you can make a decreasing GST adjustment on your BAS when you write the debt off as bad or it has been overdue for 12 months or more (the adjustment is 1/11 of the amount). If you later recover it, you make an increasing adjustment. The ATO also lets businesses claim a deduction for bad debts that were previously included in assessable income — talk to your tax agent about your situation. Cash-basis GST reporters don't need the adjustment because GST was never reported on the unpaid amount.

Stop it happening next time

  • Put clear payment terms on every quote and invoice: due date, methods, deposit, and what happens if payment is late (including any interest).
  • Take a deposit on bigger jobs (QLD domestic building work has deposit caps — see the QBCC $3,300 and deposits guide).
  • Invoice the same day the job finishes, and chase on day 1 overdue — not day 30.
  • Keep a single list of who owes what and how many days overdue, so nothing slides.

Worked example (made-up numbers — example only)

A Brisbane mobile welder (not GST-registered) invoices a farm business A$2,640 for gate and yard repairs, 14-day terms. Day 1 overdue: friendly text and email with the invoice. Day 10: second reminder with a pay-by date. Day 24: final notice. Day 31: letter of demand by registered post, 7 days to pay, stating a QCAT minor debt claim will be lodged otherwise. Still unpaid, the welder lodges a QCAT minor debt application for A$2,640 plus the filing fee, attaches the accepted quote, invoice, reminders and delivery card, and serves the application. Every step is logged with dates.

Common questions

How long should I wait before chasing an unpaid invoice?

Chase on the first day after the due date with a friendly reminder — a missed payment is often an oversight. A common rhythm is a reminder on day 1 overdue, a second reminder around day 7–14, a final notice, then a letter of demand. Your written payment terms set the due date, so put them on every quote and invoice.

Can I charge interest on a late invoice?

Only claim late-payment interest if it was agreed in your contract or terms. business.gov.au says to include an interest rate in a letter of demand only if it was specified in the contract. In a QCAT minor debt claim with no agreed rate, the tribunal can decide interest.

Can I take a customer to QCAT for an unpaid invoice?

In Queensland, QCAT hears minor debt disputes for a fixed or agreed amount up to $25,000 (excluding interest), where there was an agreement about payment (an unpaid invoice counts) made no more than 6 years ago. Above $25,000 you can reduce the claim to $25,000 or go to the Magistrates Court (up to $150,000). You must serve a copy of the application on the respondent.

Do I get the GST back if a customer never pays?

If you are GST-registered and account for GST on a non-cash (accruals) basis, you can make a decreasing adjustment on your BAS when you write the debt off as bad or it has been overdue for 12 months or more. If you account on a cash basis you only report GST when paid, so no adjustment is needed. If you later recover the money, make an increasing adjustment.
