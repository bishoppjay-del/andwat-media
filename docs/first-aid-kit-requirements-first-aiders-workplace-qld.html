First aid at work in QLD: kits, first aider ratios and training

Last reviewed 22 November 2026 · Sources linked below · Written by Jay at Andwat Co, an Australian boilermaker/fabricator (Cert III Engineering, 12+ years in the trade). About

What WHS Regulation s42 and the First aid in the workplace Code of Practice 2021 require: first aider ratios (1:50, 1:25, 1:10), CPR every year, first aid every 3 years, kit contents and the 12-month check.

Every Queensland business with workers has a legal duty to provide first aid. The law itself is short: section 42 of the WHS Regulation. The detail (how many first aiders, what goes in the kit, how often it gets checked) is in the First aid in the workplace Code of Practice 2021. Here's what it says, in plain English, for fab shops, crews and small trade businesses.

The short answer

  • The duty (WHS Regulation s42): the PCBU must provide first aid equipment, make sure every worker can get to it, provide access to facilities for giving first aid, and make sure there are enough trained first aiders (your own workers or other trained people workers can access).
  • How much is "enough" depends on the nature of the work and hazards, the size and location of the workplace, and the number and type of workers and other people there (s42(3)).
  • Recommended first aider ratios (Code of Practice): 1 per 50 workers in low risk workplaces, 1 per 25 in high risk workplaces (for example construction), and 1 per 10 in remote high risk workplaces.
  • Training: as a minimum, the nationally recognised unit Provide First Aid (or equivalent) from an RTO. CPR refresher every year; first aid qualification renewed every 3 years.
  • Kits: at least one kit every worker can get to, checked after each use or at least every 12 months, with a signed and dated contents list.

What the kit should look like

  • Easy to spot: a white cross on a green background on the outside.
  • Big enough for the contents, protects them from dust, moisture and contamination, and has a contents list inside.
  • Kept somewhere prominent and close to higher-risk areas (in a workshop, near the machines and welding bays, not locked in the office).
  • Mobile workers whose vehicle is their workplace should have a portable kit in the vehicle, secured so it can't become a projectile in a crash.

The Code's example contents (Appendix E) include a CPR flow chart, adhesive strips, eye pads, triangular, crepe and conforming bandages, wound/combine and non-adherent dressings, gauze, tweezers, scissors, disposable gloves, a sharps container, sterile saline or sterile water for eye wash, a resuscitation mask, antiseptic, an ice pack, an emergency rescue blanket, and access to 20 minutes of clean running water (or hydrogel sachets) for burns. The Code recommends more eye pads where there's welding, cutting, machining or flying particles. Painkillers like paracetamol and aspirin shouldn't go in the kit.

Keeping the kit legal: a 5-minute check

  1. Nominate one person (usually your first aider) to look after each kit.
  2. Replace anything used as soon as practicable.
  3. Check the kit after each use, and at least every 12 months if it isn't used.
  4. Check expiry dates, sterile packs still sealed, nothing damaged.
  5. Sign and date the contents list after every check.
  6. Consider an AED if there's a risk of electrocution, the ambulance would be delayed getting to you, or there are large numbers of the public (the Code's examples).

FAQ

How many first aiders do I need in Queensland?

The Code of Practice recommends one first aider for every 50 workers in low risk workplaces, one for every 25 in high risk workplaces such as construction, and one for every 10 in remote high risk workplaces. Your actual number should come from your own risk assessment.

How often does first aid training need to be renewed?

The First aid in the workplace Code of Practice 2021 says CPR refresher training should be done annually and first aid qualifications renewed every three years.

Is a first aid kit a legal requirement?

Yes. WHS Regulation section 42 requires the PCBU to provide first aid equipment and make sure each worker can access it. In practice that means at least one first aid kit.

How often should a workplace first aid kit be checked?

After each use or, if it isn't used, at least once every 12 months. The inventory list in the kit should be signed and dated after each check.

Can I put Panadol in the work first aid kit?

The Code says medication such as paracetamol and aspirin should not be included in first aid kits. Workers should carry their own medication.
