Fire extinguisher servicing in QLD: 6-monthly checks, records and AS 1851

Last reviewed 27 November 2026 · Sources linked below · Written by Jay at Andwat Co, an Australian boilermaker/fabricator (Cert III Engineering, 12+ years in the trade). About

What the Building Fire Safety Regulation 2008 asks a QLD workshop occupier to do for fire extinguishers: who services them, 6-monthly and 5-yearly intervals, the s 55 record, critical defects and 2-year record keeping.

The tag on the extinguisher by the welding bay says it was "done" in March. Is that enough if a fire officer walks in? In Queensland, no: Queensland fire guidance says the maintenance tag, invoice or summary report isn't accepted as the record of maintenance on its own. Here's what the Building Fire Safety Regulation 2008 actually asks the occupier of a workshop or commercial building to do for prescribed fire extinguishers, and how often they get serviced.

The short answer

  • Who: the occupier of the building must make sure each prescribed fire safety installation is maintained by an appropriately qualified person (Building Fire Safety Regulation 2008, s 54).
  • How often: at the intervals in the Queensland Development Code part MP6.1 (s 54). Queensland fire guidance lists extinguishers as 6-monthly inspection and test, plus a 5-yearly pressure test, under QDC MP6.1 and AS 1851 (fire safety management plan guide).
  • Fix it: if the record shows a repair or corrective action is needed, the occupier must get it done within 1 month of the maintenance, unless there's a reasonable excuse (s 54(4)).
  • Record it: the occupier must keep a record of maintenance with the details listed in s 55. A tag or an invoice isn't the record.
  • Keep it: fire safety records and prescribed documents (which include records of maintenance) are kept for at least 2 years and produced to an authorised fire officer on request (Fire Safety Management Tool advisory notes).

Which extinguishers this applies to

The regulation is about prescribed fire safety installations. Queensland fire guidance explains that prescribed fire extinguishers are the ones required to be installed in the building. Which ones those are depends on the building's approval and the building code, so check your building approval documents or ask your fire services contractor. Extra extinguishers you carry for hot work on site, in the ute or by the welding bay are still worth servicing on the same cycle: an extinguisher that doesn't discharge is no use either way.

What the record of maintenance must show (s 55)

ItemIn plain English
DescriptionWhich installation was maintained (e.g. each extinguisher by location and type).
Who did itName and licence number of the appropriately qualified person, or of the person supervising if someone else did the work.
DateThe maintenance date.
What was doneA brief description of the maintenance.
StandardThat it was carried out in compliance with QDC part MP6.1.
ResultsThe results, including whether it passed, and any repair or corrective action needed.
Sign-offA statement signed by the person who did the maintenance certifying the record is correct, plus any critical defect notice (Queensland fire guidance, item 8 of the record checklist).

Critical defects: the 24-hour rule

A critical defect is one likely to make the installation inoperable and reasonably likely to have a significant adverse impact on occupant safety (s 49). The person doing the maintenance must give the occupier a critical defect notice in the approved form within 24 hours (s 53). The regulation's own example says one dead extinguisher out of several in part of a building is not a critical defect, but it still has to be fixed within the 1-month window.

A simple workshop routine

  1. List every extinguisher by location, type and size, plus fire blankets and hose reels if fitted.
  2. Book the 6-monthly service with a licensed fire protection contractor and diarise the next one. Note which units are due their 5-yearly pressure test.
  3. Ask for the full record of maintenance (not just new tags) and file it with the job's WHS records for at least 2 years.
  4. Track any failed or removed units until they're replaced, and log the date the fix was done.
  5. Between services, keep the access clear. A blocked or missing extinguisher near hot work is a problem on the day, not at the next service.

FAQ

How often do fire extinguishers need servicing in Queensland?

At the intervals in Queensland Development Code part MP6.1, which the Building Fire Safety Regulation 2008 requires the occupier to follow. Queensland fire guidance lists extinguishers as a 6-monthly inspection and test plus a 5-yearly pressure test under QDC MP6.1 and AS 1851, and its record checklist refers to 6-monthly, yearly and 5-yearly maintenance as appropriate.

Is the tag on the extinguisher enough proof of servicing?

No. Queensland fire guidance says the maintenance tag, invoice or summary report are not acceptable as a record of maintenance, although they may show routine service is happening. The record must include the details in section 55 of the Building Fire Safety Regulation 2008, including the technician's name and licence number and a signed statement.

How long do I keep fire extinguisher service records?

Queensland fire guidance says the occupier must keep fire safety records and prescribed documents, which include records of maintenance, for at least 2 years and produce them to an authorised fire officer on request.

Can I service my own workshop extinguishers?

Not the prescribed ones. Section 54 of the Building Fire Safety Regulation 2008 says the occupier must ensure maintenance of each prescribed fire safety installation is carried out by an appropriately qualified person, and the record must show that person's name and licence number.
