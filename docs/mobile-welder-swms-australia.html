Mobile welder SWMS: which high-risk construction work triggers apply

Last reviewed 28 October 2026 · Sources linked below · Written by Jay at Andwat Co, an Australian boilermaker/fabricator (Cert III Engineering, 12+ years in the trade). About

Which of the 18 high risk construction work types a mobile or ute welder commonly hits, what to put in the SWMS, and how it sits with a hot work permit. Sourced from Safe Work Australia and WorkSafe Queensland.

If you weld, grind or cut on someone else's site, the principal contractor will usually ask for a safe work method statement (SWMS) before you strike an arc. The general SWMS rules are covered in our high risk construction work guide. This page is the mobile-welder version: which of the 18 high-risk triggers a ute welder commonly hits, and how to write a SWMS that a site will accept.

The short answer

  • A SWMS is required when the work is high risk construction work under the model WHS Regulations. Safe Work Australia lists 18 types.
  • Mobile welders most often trigger: work with a risk of falling more than 2 m, work near energised electrical services, movement of powered mobile plant nearby, work in or near a confined space, work in a contaminated or flammable atmosphere, and work adjacent to traffic.
  • The SWMS must name the high-risk activities, the hazards, and the controls you will use. The principal contractor needs a copy before the work starts.
  • A generic "welding SWMS" that ignores the actual site (heights, tanks, plant, traffic) gets knocked back. Write it for the job in front of you.

Which HRCW boxes a mobile welder usually ticks

Use this as a checklist against Safe Work Australia's list, not as legal advice:

  • Fall more than 2 m – welding from a scissor lift, EWP, scaffold or roof edge.
  • Energised electrical installations or services – welding near live switchboards, underground services or overhead lines, or working from a generator on a wet slab.
  • Powered mobile plant moving – forklifts, EWPs, cranes or excavators sharing the area.
  • Confined space – tanks, vessels, pits, ducts or pipework you enter (needs a confined space entry permit as well).
  • Contaminated or flammable atmosphere – welding on or near tanks, drums, fuel lines, paint stores or vapor areas. Pair this with a hot work permit.
  • Traffic corridor – roadside, rail corridor or active driveway work.
  • Temporary support to prevent collapse – cutting members that still hold load.

If none of the 18 apply, a SWMS is not the WHS trigger, but many sites still want a method statement as a condition of entry. Follow the site rules.

What to put in a mobile welding SWMS

  • The exact high-risk construction work types that apply.
  • The sequence: arrive, induct, isolate, set exclusion zone, prep metal, weld/grind/cut, cool-down / fire watch, pack up.
  • Hazards for each step: sparks, fume, UV, electric shock, falls, plant collision, fire, gas bottles tipping.
  • Controls that are real on a ute job: fire blankets and extinguisher, flash screens, fume extraction or position upwind, RCD on the lead, bottle restraints, spotter when plant is moving, edge protection or harness where required, hot work permit where the site asks for it.
  • Who is responsible for each control, and how the crew is told (toolbox talk + induction).
  • How you will review it if the job changes (move to a tank, go up on a roof, plant arrives).

Questions the other guides don't answer

I'm a sole trader with one ute. Do I still write a SWMS?

Yes, if the work is high risk construction work. The duty sits with the PCBU doing the work, and a sole trader is still a PCBU. Keep it short and job-specific.

Can I reuse last month's SWMS?

Only if the hazards and controls still match. Change of location, height, plant, tank contents or weather means a review. Sites often want the SWMS dated for this job.

Who gets a copy?

The principal contractor for the construction project, and the workers who will do the work. Keep it available on site while the work is happening.

Does a hot work permit replace the SWMS?

No. A hot work permit is the site's fire-control paperwork for that shift. A SWMS covers the high-risk construction method for the job. Many sites want both.

Quick answers

When does a mobile welder need a SWMS in Australia?

When the welding, grinding or cutting is high risk construction work under the WHS Regulations (one of the 18 types listed by Safe Work Australia), the PCBU must prepare, keep, comply with and review a SWMS and give a copy to the principal contractor.

Which high risk construction work types do mobile welders commonly trigger?

Common triggers are risk of falling more than 2 m, work near energised electrical services, movement of powered mobile plant, confined spaces, contaminated or flammable atmospheres, and work adjacent to traffic corridors.

Does a hot work permit replace a SWMS?

No. A hot work permit manages fire risk for a shift or task. A SWMS covers the high-risk construction work method. Many sites require both.

Can a sole trader use a template SWMS?

Yes as a starting point, but it must be reviewed against the actual site hazards and controls before work starts. A generic document that ignores heights, plant or tanks often gets rejected.
