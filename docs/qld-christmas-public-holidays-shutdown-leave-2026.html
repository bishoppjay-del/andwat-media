QLD Christmas public holidays 2026 and the shutdown: pay and leave for tradies

Last reviewed 16 November 2026 · Sources linked below · Written by Jay at Andwat Co, an Australian boilermaker/fabricator (Cert III Engineering, 12+ years in the trade). About

Queensland's 2026–27 Christmas and New Year public holidays (including Monday 28 December and the Christmas Eve 6pm part-day), who gets paid for them, and the 28-day written notice rule for directing staff to take annual leave over a shutdown.

Every year the same questions come up in the lunch shed before the break: is Boxing Day on a Saturday still a day off, do I get paid for Christmas if the shop's shut anyway, and can the boss make me burn my annual leave? In 2026 the dates fall awkwardly, so it's worth getting them right before you plan the holidays or the last pay run.

This page is for Queensland apprentices, employed tradies and small trade and fab businesses with a few staff. It's general information summarised from the Queensland Government, the Fair Work Ombudsman and the Fair Work Act sources linked below, not legal or workplace relations advice. Sole traders and subbies working on an ABN don't get public holiday pay or annual leave from anyone; the Christmas cash flow guide is the one for you.

Queensland public holidays, Christmas 2026 to New Year 2027

From the Queensland Government's public holidays page:

Christmas Eve (part-day, 6pm to midnight)Thursday 24 December 2026
Christmas DayFriday 25 December 2026
Boxing DaySaturday 26 December 2026 and Monday 28 December 2026
New Year's DayFriday 1 January 2027

Two things trip people up this year:

  • Monday 28 December is a public holiday in Queensland. Because Boxing Day falls on a Saturday, the Holidays Act 1983 (Qld) adds an extra public holiday on the Monday. The Queensland page lists both the Saturday and the Monday.
  • Christmas Eve is a part-day public holiday from 6pm to midnight (in place since 2019). It only matters if you normally work, or are asked to work, on the Thursday evening.

For a normal Monday-to-Friday crew that means three weekday public holidays over the break: Friday 25 December, Monday 28 December and Friday 1 January.

Who gets paid for a public holiday you don't work

Public holiday pay comes from the National Employment Standards (NES) in the Fair Work Act 2009. Section 116 says an employee who is absent on a public holiday must be paid at their base rate of pay for their ordinary hours of work on that day. The Fair Work Ombudsman puts it plainly:

  • Full-time and part-time employees who would normally work on that day get their base pay rate for the ordinary hours they would have worked.
  • Base rate means base rate. It doesn't include overtime, penalty rates, loadings, allowances, bonuses or incentive payments. If you normally do a 10-hour day with 2 hours of overtime, you're paid the ordinary hours only.
  • No ordinary hours, no payment. A part-timer who doesn't normally work Fridays isn't paid for Christmas Day or New Year's Day this year. A casual who isn't rostered on isn't paid for the day.
  • Rosters can't be juggled to dodge it. The Ombudsman says an employee's roster can't be changed to deliberately avoid public holiday pay.
  • Saturday workers: both Boxing Day dates are public holidays, so if your ordinary hours include Saturdays, check with your award or the Fair Work Infoline how they apply to you.

Public holidays that fall inside your annual leave

This is the one that saves people leave every year. The Fair Work Ombudsman says that if a public holiday falls during paid annual leave (or sick leave), the employee is paid for the public holiday, and that payment doesn't come out of their leave balance, as long as the public holiday falls on a day they would usually work.

Worked example. A full-time boilermaker works Monday to Friday. The shop shuts from Monday 21 December 2026 and reopens Monday 4 January 2027. That's 10 weekdays. Three of them (25 December, 28 December and 1 January) are public holidays, paid as public holidays. So the shutdown should use 7 days of annual leave, not 10. Check your payslip after the break.

Can the boss make you take annual leave over the shutdown?

It depends on your award or enterprise agreement. The Fair Work Ombudsman's position:

  • Award or agreement employees can be directed to take annual leave during a shutdown only if their award or agreement allows it. If it has no shutdown rule, the employer can't direct it, but the two can agree on annual leave (including leave in advance) or unpaid leave.
  • Many awards changed from 1 May 2023, and the Ombudsman names building and construction among the industries affected. Under the updated rules the employer may require paid annual leave during a temporary shutdown, must give at least 28 days' written notice to everyone affected, and the requirement must be reasonable. The notice period can be shortened by agreement between the employer and the majority of affected employees.
  • Not enough leave saved up? The employee can agree with the employer to use accrued time off, take annual leave in advance, or take leave without pay for the days not covered.
  • Award and agreement free employees can be required to take paid annual leave if the requirement is reasonable, which the Ombudsman says includes a temporary shutdown between Christmas and New Year.
  • Only part of the business closing? Generally, only staff in the part that's shut can be made to take leave. People in a section still working can't, unless the award or agreement says otherwise.

Counting back the 28 days

If 28 days' written notice applies to you, count back from the first day of the shutdown. A shutdown starting Monday 21 December 2026 needs the notice in writing by Monday 23 November 2026. One starting Thursday 24 December needs it by Thursday 26 November. If you run a small shop and haven't sent the email yet, this week is the time. The Fair Work Ombudsman has a template letter for directing employees to take annual leave during a shutdown.

Working through the break

  • An employer can ask you to work a public holiday, but the request has to be reasonable, and you can refuse if the request is unreasonable or your refusal is reasonable. Section 114 lists what counts, including the type of work, your family responsibilities, whether you could expect the request, the pay and penalty rates on offer, and how much notice you were given.
  • If you do work it, your award or agreement sets any penalty rate, day in lieu or extra leave. Check the Fair Work pay guides for your award rather than relying on what the shed says.
  • The Ombudsman says staff who keep working through a shutdown, such as a skeleton crew, get their normal pay, and a public holiday in that time is either a paid day off or paid at the award's public holiday rate if worked.
  • Talk before the break about whether anyone has to stay contactable or on call.

Storms over the break: shutdown is not a stand down

Queensland's storm season runs straight through Christmas. The Fair Work Ombudsman separates a shutdown (the business chooses to close, such as over Christmas and New Year) from a stand down (staff can't be usefully employed for reasons outside the employer's control, such as equipment breakdown the employer isn't responsible for, or a natural disaster). Different rules apply to each, and many construction awards have their own inclement weather clauses. See the Ombudsman's stand downs and severe weather pages linked below, and the QLD Christmas shutdown and storm checklist for making the site safe before you lock the gate.

Quick checklist for small trade employers

  • Find your award (most site trades: building and construction; most fab shops: manufacturing) or your enterprise agreement, and read its shutdown clause.
  • Pick the shutdown dates and send written notice to everyone affected, at least 28 days ahead if your award requires it.
  • Check each person's leave balance and agree in writing on leave in advance or unpaid leave for anyone short.
  • Mark 25 December, 28 December and 1 January as public holidays in the pay run, not as annual leave.
  • Remember Payday Super: public holidays aren't business days, so the 7 business day super window stretches over the break. See the Christmas bonuses guide.
  • Keep the notice, the replies and the leave records with your wage records.

Questions people ask

What are the Queensland public holidays over Christmas 2026?

The Queensland Government lists Christmas Eve from 6pm to midnight on Thursday 24 December 2026, Christmas Day on Friday 25 December, Boxing Day on Saturday 26 December plus an extra holiday on Monday 28 December, and New Year's Day on Friday 1 January 2027.

Do I get paid for a public holiday during the Christmas shutdown?

Under the National Employment Standards, full-time and part-time employees who would normally work on the day get their base pay rate for the ordinary hours they would have worked. If the public holiday falls during paid annual leave, it is paid as a public holiday and doesn't come out of the leave balance. Casuals not rostered on and part-timers who don't normally work that day aren't paid for it.

How much notice do I need to give staff for a Christmas shutdown?

Under the many awards with the updated shutdown rules (including building and construction), the Fair Work Ombudsman says an employer must give at least 28 days' written notice of the shutdown, and the requirement to take annual leave must be reasonable. The notice period can be shortened by agreement with the majority of affected employees. Check your own award or enterprise agreement.
