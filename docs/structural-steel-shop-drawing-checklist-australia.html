Structural steel shop drawing checklist (AU): what to check before fabrication

Last reviewed 27 November 2026 · Sources linked below · Written by Jay at Andwat Co, an Australian boilermaker/fabricator (Cert III Engineering, 12+ years in the trade). About

What to check on structural steel shop drawings before you cut: approval, construction category, grades, bolts, weld symbols, coating, galv holes, lifting and erection info. AS/NZS 5131 and ASI sources.

A lot of fab shop rework starts on the shop drawing before anyone picks up a grinder: the wrong revision, a bolt category nobody queried, a capped SHS with no vent holes, a heavy rafter with no lifting lugs. A check at the drawing stage is cheap. Fixing steel that's already cut, welded and coated isn't. Here's a practical checklist for Australian fabricators, estimators and builders' reps, using AS/NZS 5131 and the Australian Steel Institute (ASI) guidance.

The short answer

  • Don't cut until the drawings are approved. ASI's Structural Steelwork Standard Drawing Notes (2nd edition, 2020) include a clause that shop drawings are submitted for approval and "no steelwork shall be fabricated until final approval of the shop detail drawings has been received and all review comments on the workshop drawings have been resolved."
  • Know who approves them. ASI's guide for builders lists "Preparation of the shop detailing documentation" and "Approval of shop detailer documentation" as separate AS/NZS 5131 responsibilities that should be assigned for each project, preferably in the contract documents.
  • Check the construction category first. It drives traceability, tolerances and inspection. ASI says that if no category is shown, AS 4100 requires the default of CC2.
  • Then check the sheet itself: revision, materials and grades, set-out, holes and bolts, welds, coating, galv vent holes, lifting and erection information, piece marks.

Step 1: confirm approval status and revision

AS/NZS 5131:2016, Structural steelwork – Fabrication and erection, is the standard most Australian structural steel specs now call up. ASI built its National Structural Steelwork Compliance Scheme and standard drawing notes around it. Practical checks before anything else:

  1. The sheet is stamped or marked as approved (or approved subject to the corrections noted), and you have the approval in writing, by email or transmittal.
  2. Every review comment is closed out on the revision you're about to build from. "Approved subject to comments" with the comments still open is not a cut-ready drawing.
  3. The shop drawing revision matches the current engineer's and architect's drawings. Check the drawing register, not the PDF someone forwarded last month.
  4. Anything still unresolved is flagged HOLD on the sheet and logged as an RFI with the answer recorded.

Step 2: construction category, traceability and tolerances

Under AS/NZS 5131 the engineer assigns a Construction Category from CC1 to CC4. ASI's builder guide says CC1 is the lowest risk and CC4 the highest, most structures in Australia would be CC2 (or CC3 where fatigue governs, such as road and rail bridges), and "if no Construction Category is indicated, then AS 4100 requires the default of CC2 be used." A structure can have more than one category, so check for components called out at a higher one.

The category changes what the shop has to record. The default traceability table in ASI's standard drawing notes:

Construction CategoryType of traceabilityExtent (ASI standard drawing notes default)
CC1Not requiredNot applicable
CC2LotMain structural members, connections between main structural members and major plate components
CC3LotAll items, including cleats, brackets and the like
CC4LotAll items, including cleats, brackets and the like

The same notes set functional tolerances at Class 1 unless noted otherwise, and say AS/NZS 5131 suggests Class 2 should be considered for CC3 and CC4. Your project's own drawing notes and spec override these defaults, so read them.

Step 3: the sheet-by-sheet checklist

Title block and references

  • Drawing number, revision, date and a description of what changed in each revision.
  • References back to the engineer's drawing numbers and gridlines the piece comes from.
  • Drafting follows the usual Australian conventions: AS 1100.101 (general principles) and AS/NZS 1100.501:2002, Technical drawing, Part 501: Structural engineering drawing.

Materials and grades

  • Every section size, plate thickness and grade matches the engineer's drawings. Don't assume a grade; check the general notes.
  • The supply standard is right for the product: hot-rolled bars and sections are commonly specified to AS/NZS 3679.1 and cold-formed hollow sections to AS/NZS 1163.
  • Section sizes are ones you can actually buy. Flag any odd size early, because a substitution needs the engineer's approval.

Dimensions and set-out

  • Overall lengths, setting-out points and levels tie back to the project grid.
  • Holding-down bolt and anchor plans match the base plates you're drawing.
  • Camber or pre-set is shown where the engineer calls for it.
  • Add up the dimension strings and check they match the overall length. A string that doesn't add up is an easy slip to miss.

Holes and bolts

  • Bolt diameter, property class and bolting category (for example 8.8/S snug-tight vs 8.8/TB or 8.8/TF fully tensioned) are stated for every connection. The tensioned categories change the installation and inspection, so query anything unclear.
  • High-strength structural bolt assemblies are commonly specified to AS/NZS 1252.1.
  • Hole sizes, edge distances and pitches match the engineer's connection design or the standard connections the notes refer to.
  • If the engineer used ASI's standard drawing notes, check the "unless noted otherwise" minimums in them. The ASI template lists, for example, 6 mm continuous fillet welds all round, a minimum of two M20 grade 8.8/S bolts per steel-to-steel bolted connection, at least two threads past the nut, 10 mm minimum plates and 8 mm minimum purlin cleats. Those are template defaults: the project's own notes can change them.

Welds

  • Weld symbols are complete: type, size, length, all-round or field-weld flags. The symbols standard is AS 1101.3, Graphical symbols for general engineering, Part 3: Welding and non-destructive examination; Standards Australia lists the 2026 edition as current.
  • The weld category (SP or GP) and any NDE are stated, consistent with AS/NZS 1554.1 and the spec. See WPS vs PQR under AS/NZS 1554.1 for the procedure side.
  • Site welds are shown on the drawing. ASI's template notes say other than site welds shown on the shop drawings, don't weld on site without prior approval, and to locate site welds for down-hand welding where possible.

Coating and galvanizing

  • Surface preparation and the full coating system are stated, plus any surfaces to be masked (faying surfaces on friction-grip connections, site-weld zones).
  • For hot dip galvanizing: every hollow section is vented and drained at both ends, end plates and stiffeners are sniped, and assemblies fit your galvanizer's bath. ASI's notes include a clause to detail steelwork to the available bath dimensions. Hole sizes are in the galvanizing vent and drain hole guide.

Handling, lifting and erection

  • Piece mass is shown for heavy members, and lifting lugs or lifting points are detailed where needed.
  • Erection marks, orientation (which end is north or up) and temporary bracing or erection cleats are shown.
  • Splices are placed so each piece can be transported and lifted in one go.

Piece marks and the cut list

  • Every piece has a unique mark, and the marks match the material schedule and cut list. See the steel material schedule (BOM) guide.
  • Quantities on the drawing match the quantities on the takeoff. Do your takeoff from the approved revision, not the tender set (see how to do a structural steel takeoff).

Why the lifting and bracing info matters: WHS safe design

Under section 22 of the Work Health and Safety Act 2011 (Qld), a business that designs a structure that will be used as a workplace must ensure, so far as is reasonably practicable, that it is designed to be without risks to health and safety, including for the people constructing it. WorkSafe QLD's Safe design of structures Code of Practice 2021 (varied 1 September 2026) says designers must give adequate information to each person provided with the design, and lists points to consider, including:

  • making notes on drawings, "as these will be immediately available to construction workers"
  • "heavy or awkward prefabricated elements likely to create handling risks"
  • "temporary work required to construct or renovate the building as designed (e.g. bracing of steel or concrete frame buildings)"

Section 295 of the Work Health and Safety Regulation 2011 (Qld) also requires a designer of a structure to give the business that commissioned the design a written safety report on hazards specific to that design. The Code includes a worked example where a designer reviewed a stair design with the steelwork fabricator so the stair flights could be pre-assembled on the ground and lifted in whole, cutting time spent working at height.

In practice: if the shop drawing has a heavy, awkward piece with no lifting points, no mass and no bracing note, that's a gap to raise before fabrication, not on site. Whether a particular shop detailer counts as a "designer" under the WHS Act depends on the facts. Get advice if it matters on your job.

FAQ

Can I start fabricating before the shop drawings are approved?

Check your contract first. The Australian Steel Institute's standard drawing notes include a clause that shop drawings are submitted for approval and no steelwork is fabricated until final approval is received and all review comments are resolved. ASI also recommends naming who prepares and who approves shop drawings in the contract documents. If you cut before approval and the drawing changes, the rework is usually yours.

Who approves structural steel shop drawings in Australia?

Whoever the contract or construction specification names. ASI's guide for builders lists preparing the shop detailing documentation and approving it as separate responsibilities under AS/NZS 5131 that should be assigned on a project-specific basis, preferably in the contract documents. Get the approval in writing and keep it with the job's QA records.

What construction category applies if the drawings don't show one?

ASI's National Structural Steelwork Compliance Scheme guide says that if no Construction Category is indicated, AS 4100 requires the default of CC2. It also says most structures in Australia would be CC2. The engineer assigns the category, so raise an RFI if it's missing.

Which standard covers weld symbols on Australian drawings?

AS 1101.3, Graphical symbols for general engineering, Part 3: Welding and non-destructive examination. Standards Australia lists AS 1101.3:2026 as the current edition, replacing AS 1101.3-2005. Weld categories (SP or GP) and weld quality come from AS/NZS 1554.1 and the project specification.
