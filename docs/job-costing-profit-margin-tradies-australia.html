Job costing for tradies: how to tell if a job actually made money

Last reviewed 21 November 2026 · Sources linked below · Written by Jay at Andwat Co, an Australian boilermaker/fabricator (Cert III Engineering, 12+ years in the trade). About

How Australian tradies can cost each job: quote vs actual labour, materials and consumables, a loaded labour rate with 12% super, margin vs markup, a worked steel job example and how to fix your quotes.

Most tradies know what they quoted. Far fewer know what the job actually cost them once the extra trip to the supplier, the re-cut, the gas bottle and the Saturday morning are counted. Job costing is just writing those numbers down against each job, then comparing them with the quote. Here's the simple version that works for a one- or two-person business.

The short answer

  • Job costing = recording the real labour, materials, consumables, subbies and travel for each job, then comparing them with what you quoted.
  • Gross profit = price (ex GST) minus the job's direct costs. Margin = gross profit ÷ price. Markup = gross profit ÷ cost. They are not the same number.
  • Cost your labour at a loaded rate, not the bare wage: wage plus super (the super guarantee is 12% of ordinary time earnings from 1 July 2025), workers' comp, leave and non-billable time.
  • Do it on every job, even small ones. Patterns (always over on hours, always under on consumables) show up after 5 to 10 jobs.

Margin vs markup: the mistake that eats profit

If you add 25% to your costs, you have a 25% markup but only a 20% margin. On a job that costs A$4,000, a 25% markup gives a A$5,000 price and A$1,000 gross profit, which is 20% of the price. If you think you're "making 25%", you're over-estimating your profit by a quarter.

Formula: margin = markup ÷ (1 + markup). Going the other way, markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin).

What to record for each job

  1. Quote: price ex GST, estimated hours, estimated materials.
  2. Labour: actual hours per person (including travel and rework if you're not charging it) × loaded hourly cost.
  3. Materials: supplier invoices or dockets for that job, including offcuts you can't reuse.
  4. Consumables: wire, rods, discs, gas, fixings, sealant. Use a fair allowance per job if you can't track each item.
  5. Subbies and hire: crane, EWP, galvanising, powder coat, delivery.
  6. Travel: fuel, tolls, parking.
  7. Variations: anything extra the client asked for, and whether it was invoiced.

Keep the dockets and invoices. The ATO expects business records to be kept for five years, and the same paperwork backs up your costing.

Working out your loaded labour cost

For an employee, add up a year of wages, super, workers' compensation premium and leave, then divide by the hours you can actually bill (not the hours paid). For yourself as a sole trader, start from what you need to take home each year plus your own super and insurance, and divide by realistic billable hours. Many small operators only bill 60–75% of the hours they work once quoting, driving, pickups and admin are counted. The charge-out rate calculator walks through the same maths.

Turn the numbers into better quotes

  • After 5–10 jobs, compare quoted vs actual hours by job type and adjust your estimating allowance.
  • Price variations in writing before you do them, and log them against the job.
  • Drop or re-price job types that keep coming in under your target margin.
  • Chase unpaid invoices early: a job hasn't made money until it's paid.

FAQ

What is job costing for tradies?

It's recording the real labour, materials, consumables, subcontractor and travel costs for each job and comparing them with the quote, so you can see the actual gross profit and margin on every job.

What's the difference between margin and markup?

Markup is profit divided by cost. Margin is profit divided by price. A 25% markup on cost is only a 20% margin on the price.

What labour rate should I use in job costing?

Use a loaded hourly cost: wages plus super (12% super guarantee from 1 July 2025), workers' compensation, leave and an allowance for non-billable time, divided by the hours you can actually bill.

How long should I keep job costing records?

The ATO generally requires business records to be kept for five years. Keep the supplier invoices, dockets and timesheets behind each job's figures.
