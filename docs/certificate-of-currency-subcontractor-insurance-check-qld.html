Certificate of currency: how to check a subcontractor's insurance (QLD)

Last reviewed 21 November 2026 · Sources linked below · Written by Jay at Andwat Co, an Australian boilermaker/fabricator (Cert III Engineering, 12+ years in the trade). About

What to check on a subcontractor's certificate of currency, when WorkCover Queensland says a subbie counts as your worker, how to check a QBCC licence, and a simple routine to track expiry dates.

Before a subbie starts on your job, most head contractors and a lot of clients will ask for a certificate of currency (COC). It's a one-page summary from the insurer saying a policy exists for a set period. It's quick to collect and quick to forget, which is why expired certificates are one of the most common gaps found when something goes wrong on site. Here's what to check, what WorkCover Queensland says about covering subcontractors, and how to stay on top of expiry dates.

The short answer

  • Ask each subbie for a current COC for public liability, and where relevant workers' compensation (if they employ people) and any contract works or professional indemnity cover your contract asks for.
  • Check the insured name and ABN match the business you're paying (use ABN Lookup), the period of insurance covers the job, and the limit meets your contract.
  • In Queensland, check the subbie's QBCC licence on the QBCC licensee register for building work over A$3,300.
  • An ABN alone doesn't make someone a contractor for workers' comp. WorkCover Queensland says a subcontractor working under a contract of service can still be your worker and need to be on your policy.
  • A COC is a snapshot. Record the expiry date and ask for the renewed one before it lapses.

Workers' comp: when a subbie counts as your worker (QLD)

WorkCover Queensland says your accident insurance policy must cover anyone who meets the definition of a worker under the Workers' Compensation and Rehabilitation Act 2003. A worker is an individual (sole traders can be workers, companies, partnerships and trusts can't) who works under a contract and is an employee for PAYG withholding purposes, or who works under a contract of service.

WorkCover Queensland sets out four tests to apply in order: is there a contract for the performance of work; is the person specifically excluded; are they specifically included; and is the arrangement a contract of service? For the last one, it points to the ATO's common law principles. Signs of a genuine contractor include running their own business and being free to take other work, deciding how the work is done, being liable for fixing their own faults, supplying their own tools, being paid for a result based on their quote, and being able to subcontract the work.

If a subbie is really working like an employee (paid by the hour, using your gear, told how and when to work), they may need to be on your policy even if they have an ABN. If you're unsure, WorkCover Queensland has worker determination tests and you can ask them directly.

QBCC licence check (Queensland)

For building work in Queensland valued over A$3,300, the contractor generally needs a QBCC licence in the right class. The QBCC licensee register shows the licence class, status, any conditions and a full history report. Check the class matches the work (for example structural steel fabrication and erection) and that the name matches the business you're contracting with.

A simple subbie onboarding routine

  1. Get the COCs, QBCC licence number, ABN and any high-risk work licences before day one.
  2. Check each one against the table above and the QBCC register.
  3. Enter the expiry dates in one register, not in your inbox.
  4. Set a reminder 30 days before each expiry and ask for the renewed certificate.
  5. Re-check if the scope of work changes (for example a subbie who was only fabricating starts erecting on site).
  6. Keep the records with your job files. You'll also need subbie payment details for the TPAR if you're in building and construction.

FAQ

What is a certificate of currency?

It's a document from an insurer confirming that a policy is in force for a stated period, with the insured name, policy type, limit and policy number. It's a snapshot, so check the expiry date.

Does a subcontractor with an ABN need to be covered by my WorkCover policy in QLD?

Possibly. WorkCover Queensland says having an ABN doesn't stop someone being a worker. If they work under a contract of service, or are an employee for PAYG withholding purposes, they need to be covered by your policy.

How do I check a contractor's QBCC licence?

Search the QBCC licensee register by licence number or name, check the licence class matches the work and download the full history to see conditions or disciplinary records.

How often should I check subcontractor insurance?

Before they start, and again before each certificate expires. Record the expiry dates in one register and ask for renewals about 30 days ahead.
