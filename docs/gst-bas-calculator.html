Free calculator for Australian sole traders and tradies: add 10% GST or take it out of a price, estimate G1, 1A and 1B for a Simpler BAS, and check whether you've hit the $75,000 GST registration threshold. Runs in your browser.

The two GST sums everyone needs

  • Adding GST to an ex-GST price: multiply by 1.1. $1,000 + 10% = $1,100, and the GST is $100.
  • Taking GST out of an inc-GST price: the GST is 1/11 of the total, not 10% of it. In $550, the GST is $50 and the ex-GST price is $500. Taking 10% off $550 gives $55, which is wrong.

What the BAS tab works out

On a Simpler BAS you report three GST figures (see our Simpler BAS guide):

  • G1 Total sales: all your sales, taxable and GST-free, either including or excluding GST. You tick which one you used.
  • 1A GST on sales: the GST in your taxable sales.
  • 1B GST on purchases: the GST credits on business purchases, counting only the business share of anything you also use privately. You need a tax invoice for purchases over $82.50 including GST.

The ATO asks for whole dollars, rounded down, so the calculator drops the cents. It's an estimate for one period. It doesn't handle instalments, adjustments, PAYG, fuel tax credits, input-taxed sales or mixed-supply bills.

Not registered for GST?

The box starts unticked. If you're not registered, don't add GST to your prices or show it on invoices, and you don't lodge 1A or 1B. You can still use the first tab to see how much GST is inside a bill you've paid, and the third tab to check each month whether you're close to $75,000. You reach the threshold if either your last 12 months (this month plus the previous 11) or your next 12 months (this month plus the next 11) comes to $75,000 or more. Then you have 21 days to register. See Do I need to register for GST?

How it adds up

The BAS figures are in whole dollars with the cents dropped, as the ATO asks. This is an estimate only. Your BAS uses your actual records and your accounting basis (cash or non-cash).

Your GST turnover, month by month

GST turnover is your total business income (not profit), less any GST included in it and some other sales. If you're not sure what counts, check the ATO page linked below. Leave out the sale of business assets from your projection.
