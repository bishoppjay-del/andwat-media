Christmas shutdown cash flow for QLD tradies and subbies (2026–27)

Last reviewed 14 November 2026 · Sources linked below · Written by Jay at Andwat Co, an Australian boilermaker/fabricator (Cert III Engineering, 12+ years in the trade). About

Why QLD subbies often aren't paid until late January (BIF Act business days skip 22 Dec–10 Jan), the 28 Feb BAS, and how much to set aside each pay before the break.

Most small building and fab businesses in Queensland know the site goes quiet over Christmas. What catches people is the cash: the work stops on a Friday in December, but the money from November and December can take until late January to land, while Christmas, Afterpay instalments and the December-quarter BAS all still need paying. This page lays out the dates for the 2026–27 break so you can plan the gap before you down tools.

It's written for sole traders, subbies and small fab shops. It's general information from the official sources linked below, not legal, tax or financial advice.

The short answer

  • The BIF Act clock stops. In Queensland, a “business day” under the BIF Act skips 22–24 December, 27–31 December and 2–10 January, plus weekends and public holidays. For this break, Monday 21 December 2026 is the last business day and Monday 11 January 2027 is the first.
  • Late claims get paid late. A claim given during the break is treated as given when business days restart. Under the default 10-business-day rule, even a claim given on 21 December isn't due until about 22 January 2027.
  • The December-quarter BAS is due 28 February (Monday 1 March 2027, because 28 February 2027 is a Sunday). There's no extra two weeks for quarter 2.
  • Plan for a gap of several weeks with no income and divide what you'll need across the pays you have left before the break.

Why the money stops before the work does

Schedule 2 of the Building Industry Fairness (Security of Payment) Act 2017 says a business day does not include a Saturday or Sunday, a public holiday, or any day from 22 to 24 December, 27 to 31 December or 2 to 10 January. QBCC's own holiday notice explains the effect: an invoice given during the closed period is taken to be received on the first business day after it, and the due date for payment is worked out from there. Its advice for subbies giving a claim is simple: “You may be paid later than you were expecting.”

For 2026–27 that means:

  • Last BIF Act business day of 2026: Monday 21 December 2026.
  • First BIF Act business day of 2027: Monday 11 January 2027 (10 January is a Sunday).

The default due date for a progress payment is 10 business days after the payment claim is given, unless the contract says otherwise. Contract terms can't push payment out past 25 business days for subcontracts or 15 business days for commercial building contracts (QBCC Act s67U and s67W). Count only business days and the gap gets long:

  • Claim given Monday 21 December 2026 on the 10-day default: due about Friday 22 January 2027.
  • Claim given any day from 22 December to 10 January: treated as given on 11 January, so due about Monday 25 January 2027 on the same default.
  • On a 25-business-day subcontract term, add roughly three more weeks.

Two things you can do: check your contract for a December reference date and get your claim in before 22 December, and talk to the builder or head contractor before the break about whether they intend to pay. QBCC recommends that conversation too. Our BIF Act payment claim guide covers what makes a claim valid. The BIF Act generally doesn't cover work for a resident owner on their own home, so domestic jobs run on the contract terms.

The bills that don't stop

  • Christmas itself. Gifts, the lunch, Boxing Day, travel to family. These land in December, before most of your December money arrives.
  • Afterpay and other pay-in-4. Afterpay's Australian page says Pay in 4 is four payments over six weeks: about 25% at purchase, then the rest roughly every two weeks. Anything bought on pay-in-4 from mid-December has instalments landing in January, right in the gap.
  • The December-quarter BAS. If you're registered for GST and report quarterly, the October–December BAS is due 28 February. The ATO says the usual extra two weeks for lodging online doesn't apply to quarter 2, because that date already includes a one-month extension. In 2027 it falls on a Sunday, so the deadline becomes Monday 1 March 2027. Put the GST you collect in October to December aside as it comes in, not out of February's first payments.
  • Your normal fixed costs. Rent or mortgage, ute repayments, phone, insurances and subscriptions keep running through January.

Work out what to set aside each pay

A simple way to size the gap:

  1. Pick the date your last payment before the break is likely to clear, and the date the first January payment is likely to land (use the business-day dates above and your contract terms).
  2. Add up everything due between those dates: Christmas spending, pay-in-4 instalments, fixed bills, and anything you want in the bank for the GST on the next BAS.
  3. Take off what's already set aside.
  4. Divide by the number of pays you have left before the break. If you pay yourself weekly or fortnightly from the business account, count those dates.

Amount still to cover ÷ pays left before the break = amount to set aside each pay.

That number is yours to work out. We won't guess how many pays you have left or what your Christmas costs. The point is to do the sum in November, while there are still pays left to spread it over.

A pre-break cash checklist

  • Check each contract for the December reference date and the payment terms (business days, not calendar days).
  • Send every payment claim you can before Tuesday 22 December 2026, and keep proof of when it was given.
  • Ask the builder or head contractor before the break whether they'll pay in full, and when.
  • Chase overdue invoices in the first week of December, not the last. See how to chase an unpaid invoice.
  • Move the October–December GST into a separate account as it's collected.
  • List every pay-in-4 purchase and the instalments that fall in January.
  • Lock the site down properly before you leave. Our QLD Christmas shutdown and storm checklist covers scaffolds, plant and site security.

Questions people ask

Do BIF Act business days stop over Christmas in Queensland?

Yes. Under schedule 2 of the Building Industry Fairness (Security of Payment) Act 2017, a business day does not include weekends, public holidays, or any day from 22 to 24 December, 27 to 31 December or 2 to 10 January. For the 2026–27 break, Monday 21 December 2026 is the last business day and Monday 11 January 2027 is the first.

If I send a payment claim on 21 December, when am I paid?

If the contract doesn't set a due date, the BIF Act default is 10 business days after the claim is given. Counting only business days, a claim given on Monday 21 December 2026 is not due until about Friday 22 January 2027. Check your contract, because it can set an earlier date or a later one within the legal caps.

When is the October to December BAS due?

The quarter 2 BAS (October, November and December) is due on 28 February. In 2027 that falls on a Sunday, so you have until Monday 1 March 2027. The ATO says the extra two weeks for lodging online does not apply to quarter 2, because 28 February already includes a one-month extension.
