Tradie quote template Australia: what to put on a quote, a free field list, and how acceptance works

Last reviewed 26 November 2026 · Sources linked below · Written by Jay at Andwat Co, an Australian boilermaker/fabricator (Cert III Engineering, 12+ years in the trade). About

A quote the customer accepts becomes a binding contract, so it's worth getting the document right, not just the price. Here's a field-by-field quote template you can build in Excel, Word or Google Sheets, based on business.gov.au's checklist, plus the extras that stop arguments later.

Pricing it first? Use the free charge-out rate calculator, then how to quote a job with GST for the GST maths and markup vs margin. This page is about the quote document itself.

The short answer

business.gov.au says a written quote should include at least: your business name, ABN and contact details; the customer's name and contact details; a clear description of the work; itemised and total costs (with GST if it applies); any variations and revisions discussed; payment terms such as the due date and late payment penalties; your preferred payment method; start and finish dates; the quote expiry date; and space for the customer to accept (signature and date). It also says that once a customer accepts a quote, it becomes a legally binding contract (business.gov.au – Prepare quotes).

Quote vs estimate

A quote is a fixed price for the scope you've described. An estimate is your best guess where there are unknowns you can't see yet. If you give an estimate, call it an estimate, give a range, say what would push it up, and tell the customer before you go past it. Consumer regulators, such as WA Consumer Protection, say tradespeople must get the customer's permission before doing extra work.

A quote isn't a tax invoice

When the job's done you still need an invoice. If you're GST-registered and the sale is over $82.50 including GST, your customer can ask for a tax invoice, and for sales of $1,000 or more it must show the buyer's identity or ABN (ATO – Tax invoices). See the tax invoice requirements guide.

Queensland building work: when a quote isn't enough

For domestic building work in Queensland over $3,300, the QBCC Act requires a written contract that meets Schedule 1B, and it caps deposits taken before you start on site: 10% of the contract price for a level 1 contract ($3,301 to $19,999), 5% for a level 2 contract ($20,000 or more), or 20% where more than half the contract price is for off-site work. An accepted one-page quote may not meet those requirements, so check the QBCC licence and $3,300 rule guide before you quote a home job (QBCC Act Sch 1B s 33).

After you send it

Follow up once at about three to five days, once more before it expires, and record the outcome (won, lost, why). If the job runs over or the customer adds work, write it up as a variation before you start. FAQ

What should a tradie include on a quote in Australia?

business.gov.au lists: your business name, ABN and contact details; the customer's name and contact details; a clear description of the work; itemised and total costs including GST if it applies; any variations discussed; payment terms such as the due date and late payment penalties; payment method; start and finish dates; the quote expiry date; and space for the customer's signature and date. Adding inclusions, exclusions and a variation clause avoids most disputes.

Is a quote legally binding in Australia?

Once the customer accepts it, business.gov.au says a quote becomes a legally binding contract on its terms. That's why the scope, exclusions, payment terms and expiry date matter as much as the price.

Should my quote include GST?

Only if you're registered for GST. If you are, state clearly whether the price includes GST and show the GST amount. If you're not registered, you can't charge GST.

What's the difference between a quote and an estimate?

A quote is a fixed price for a defined scope. An estimate is a best guess where there are unknowns. Label an estimate as an estimate, give a range and what would change it, and get the customer's OK before going over it.
