Interest on overdue invoices in Australia: what tradies can charge (QLD rates)

Last reviewed 23 November 2026 · Sources linked below · Written by Jay at Andwat Co, an Australian boilermaker/fabricator (Cert III Engineering, 12+ years in the trade). About

Can you charge interest on a late invoice in Australia? Only if it's agreed, except on QLD construction work, where the BIF Act and QBCC Act s67P set a statutory rate. Rates, the daily formula and wording for your terms.

An invoice is 30 days over and the customer is "sorting it out". Can you add interest? In most of Australia the honest answer is only if you agreed it up front. But if you're doing construction work in Queensland (and that includes fabricating and supplying steel for a building job), the law gives you interest on a late progress payment whether or not the contract mentions it. Here's how each case works, the current rates and the maths.

The short answer

  • Ordinary jobs and sales: charge interest only if it's in your quote, contract or written terms, agreed before the work. business.gov.au's first step on unpaid invoices is to check "what you both agreed would happen if payment was late".
  • QLD construction contracts (BIF Act s73): interest is payable on an unpaid progress payment at the greater of your contract rate and the court money-order rate, which Queensland Courts list as 10.35% a year for 1 July–31 December 2026.
  • QLD building contracts under QBCC Act s67P: the penalty rate applies instead: 10% a year plus the RBA 90-day bill rate (or a higher contract rate), for every day it's unpaid.
  • Maths: amount × annual rate × days late ÷ 365. $4,000 thirty days late at 10.35% ≈ $34.03.
  • Put it in writing before the job and state it on every invoice. Don't add a rate to a letter of demand that you never agreed.

1. Ordinary jobs: interest has to be agreed

For a normal domestic job, a retail sale or a service that isn't construction work, there's no automatic late-payment rate you can just add to the bill. Your right to interest comes from what the customer agreed to. business.gov.au tells businesses to read the contract terms for "what you both agreed would happen if payment was late", and to set payment terms and policies up front so customers are clear on timeframes. Our unpaid invoice guide and letter of demand template take the same line: put interest in the demand only if it was in the contract.

So if you want to be able to charge interest on future jobs, it needs to be in the paperwork before the customer says yes:

Example wording for your quote or terms (adjust before use):
Payment is due [14] days from the invoice date. Amounts not paid by the due date incur simple interest at [X]% per year, calculated daily from the day after the due date until paid. Accepting this quote means you agree to these payment terms.

Pick a rate you can justify and keep it reasonable. A large flat "late fee" that has nothing to do with what late payment actually costs you is the sort of term that can be challenged, so get advice before using one. Business terms aren't a substitute for a lawyer on a big contract.

2. Queensland construction work: interest under the BIF Act

The Building Industry Fairness (Security of Payment) Act 2017 applies to construction contracts in Queensland, including contracts to supply related goods and services such as fabricated steel. It generally doesn't cover domestic building work under a contract with a resident owner for the home they live in or intend to live in (s61), though it still covers subcontracts under the builder on those jobs. Section 73 does two things:

  • Due date. A progress payment is due on the day the contract says. If the contract doesn't say, it's due 10 business days after the payment claim is made. Some payment terms are void: more than 25 business days in a subcontract or construction management trade contract (QBCC Act s67U), or more than 15 business days in a commercial building contract (s67W). A "pay when paid" clause has no effect (BIF Act s74).
  • Interest. Once the payment is due and unpaid, interest is payable at the greater of (a) the rate in the contract and (b) the rate prescribed under the Civil Proceedings Act 2011 s59(3) for a money order debt.

The s59(3) rate is set by Supreme Court Practice Direction 7 of 2013: the RBA cash rate last published before each half-year, plus 6%. The Queensland Courts interest rates page lists:

PeriodCash rate+Money order rate
1 Jan – 30 Jun 20263.60%6%9.60% a year
1 Jul – 31 Dec 20264.35%6%10.35% a year

The rate resets on 1 January and 1 July, so check the Courts page for 2027 before you calculate anything that runs past 31 December.

3. Building contracts: the QBCC Act s67P penalty rate

BIF Act s73(3) says that if the construction contract is a building contract covered by section 67P of the Queensland Building and Construction Commission Act 1991, interest is at the s67P penalty rate instead. Section 67P sits in Part 4A, which the QBCC Industry Guide to Security of Payment Laws describes as regulating building contracts other than domestic building contracts. The penalty rate is:

  • 10% a year plus the annual rate for 90-day bills published by the Reserve Bank of Australia, as it applies from time to time, for each day the amount is unpaid; or
  • a higher rate if the building contract provides for one.

The 90-day bill rate moves, so look it up on the RBA statistical tables (money market interest rates) for the dates you're claiming. We haven't put a figure here because it changes.

Not sure whether your job is a "building contract", whether it's domestic, or which rate applies? That's exactly when to ask a construction lawyer or the QBCC before you put a number in writing.

How to calculate it (simple daily interest)

Interest = amount owing × annual rate × days late ÷ 365

Example 1: $4,000 progress payment, 30 days late, 10.35% a year → 4,000 × 0.1035 × 30 ÷ 365 = $34.03 (about $1.13 a day).

Example 2: $12,000, 45 days late, 10.35% a year → 12,000 × 0.1035 × 45 ÷ 365 = $153.12.

Count from the day after the due date. If a part payment comes in, recalculate on the lower balance from that date. If the period crosses 1 January or 1 July, split it and use each half-year's rate.

The examples are illustrations, not advice on your job. On a big claim, the interest is rarely the main event: the statutory payment claim and the BIF Act timeframes are. See the QLD BIF Act payment claim guide and progress claims, variations and retention for fabricators.

Checklist before you add interest to an invoice or demand

  1. Find the basis: your signed terms, or (QLD construction) BIF Act s73 / QBCC Act s67P.
  2. Confirm the due date: contract date, or 10 business days after the payment claim if the contract is silent or the term is void.
  3. Use the right rate for each period (Courts table, RBA 90-day bill rate, or contract rate if higher).
  4. Show the working: amount, rate, start date, end date, days, interest.
  5. Keep it separate from the principal on the invoice or letter, and don't round up.
  6. Keep copies of the quote, terms, invoice, payment claim and every reminder.

FAQ

Can I charge interest on an overdue invoice in Australia?

For an ordinary job or sale, plan on charging interest only if it was agreed before the work, in your quote, contract or written terms. business.gov.au tells businesses to check what was agreed would happen if payment was late, and to set payment terms up front. Queensland construction work is different: the BIF Act gives interest on unpaid progress payments even when the contract says nothing about it.

What interest rate applies to a late progress payment in Queensland?

Under BIF Act s73(2), interest on an unpaid progress payment under a construction contract is the greater of the contract rate and the rate for a money order debt under the Civil Proceedings Act 2011 s59(3). Queensland Courts list that rate as 10.35% a year for 1 July to 31 December 2026 (the 4.35% cash rate plus 6%). If the contract is a building contract covered by QBCC Act s67P, s73(3) says interest is at the s67P penalty rate instead: 10% a year plus the RBA 90-day bill rate, or a higher contract rate.

How do I calculate interest on a late invoice?

Simple daily interest: amount owing x annual rate x days late / 365. For example, $4,000 that is 30 days late at 10.35% a year is about $34.03. Work it from the day after the due date to the day you're paid, and use the rate that applied for each period if it changed.

Can I add interest to a QCAT minor debt claim?

QCAT's minor debt limit is $25,000 excluding interest, so interest sits on top of the limit. Claim only interest you can point to a basis for, such as your agreed terms or, for construction work, BIF Act s73. Check QCAT's current pages or get legal advice before you lodge.
