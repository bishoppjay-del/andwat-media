How to price commercial welding and fabrication jobs in Australia

Last reviewed 7 November 2026 · Sources linked below · Written by Jay at Andwat Co, an Australian boilermaker/fabricator (Cert III Engineering, 12+ years in the trade). About

Practical build-up for Australian fab shops and mobile welders: charge-out × labour hours, steel, consumables, coatings, travel, contingency and GST. Worked example plus links to job-costing and quote kits.

Commercial welding and structural fab quotes lose money the same way every week: labour hours that only cover arc time, steel bought as “total metres” instead of nested bars, coatings and NDT forgotten, and GST treatment that confuses the client. This guide is the shop method — build the price from charge-out, materials and risk, then present it cleanly. It is general information for Australian sole traders and small shops, not tax or industrial-relations advice. Check the ATO and Fair Work links below for your situation.

The short answer

  • Price = (honest labour hours × charge-out) + steel + consumables + coatings/NDT + travel/site extras + contingency, then apply GST correctly if you are registered.
  • Charge-out must cover wage or drawings, overhead, unbillable time and profit — not just “what the bloke next door charges”. Use the charge-out rate calculator.
  • Labour hours include setup, fit-up, weld, grind, assembly, QA and packing — not only weld inches or metres.
  • Materials come from a real takeoff and cut list (stock lengths, kerf, trim), not a napkin total. See the structural steel takeoff guide and cut list calculator.
  • GST: registered businesses generally include GST on taxable sales and issue tax invoices; non-registered sellers must not charge GST. See the quoting with GST and tax invoice guides.

Why underquoting kills fab shops

Typical failure modes on stairs, platforms, handrails, frames and site welding:

  • Arc-time only. The quote prices weld metres and ignores fit-up, tacking, grinding, square-and-level, bolt-up and the hour spent waiting for the crane.
  • Steel shortfall. Total linear metres look fine until you nest into 6.5 m / 7.5 m / 9 m / 12 m stock with kerf and end trim — then you are a bar short.
  • Forgotten coatings and NDT. Galvanising, paint systems, MPI or UT appear as a variation after the client already awarded the job on your low number.
  • No contingency for drawing risk. “Approx as discussed” scope eats the margin when the IFC drawings arrive thicker, with more cleats, or with site mods.
  • Winning the race to the bottom. A quote that only recovers materials and a cheap hourly rate funds the ute until the BAS, insurance or a rework week lands.

Build a charge-out that covers overhead

Your charge-out is the hourly (or day) rate you multiply by billable hours on the job. It has to recover:

  • What you pay yourself or your welders (including on-costs if you employ)
  • Workshop rent, power, insurance, vehicles, consumable stock, software, phones
  • Unbillable time (quotes, chasing steel, admin, rework that isn’t client-caused)
  • A profit margin so the business can buy plant and survive quiet months

If you employ welders or boilermakers, labour cost inputs must meet Fair Work obligations and the applicable modern award (minimum rates, loadings, overtime, allowances). Do not invent award rates — look up the award that covers your employees on the Fair Work Ombudsman site and use those figures (or your enterprise agreement) as the floor when you build charge-out. Sole traders still need a charge-out that replaces a living wage plus overhead.

Estimate labour hours honestly

Break the job into tasks and estimate hours for each — then add a small buffer for shop friction:

  1. Setup and drawing time — read IFC, mark up, program nest, cut list
  2. Cut and prep — saw/plasma, grind edges, drill/punch
  3. Fit-up and tack — square, level, gap control (often more hours than welding)
  4. Weld out — based on process (MIG/TIG/stick), joint type, position, and weld size — not a single “metres per hour” fantasy
  5. Dress and finish — grind, blend, prep for coating
  6. Assemble / trial fit — stair flights, handrail joins, base plates
  7. QA and docs — measure, photos, ITP sign-off, material certs, welder tickets if required
  8. Pack and load — still shop labour

Site welding adds access, weather, permits, fire watch and waiting on other trades. Price those as separate hour buckets or dayworks rates, not as “a bit extra on the shop rate”.

Material and consumables

  • Steel: take off designations (e.g. 310UB40.4), metres and plate kg, then buy in stock lengths with kerf and trim — see how to do a structural steel takeoff and steel cut list calculator.
  • Consumables: wire/electrodes, gas, grinding discs, tip replacements, drill bits — either a % of labour or a measured allowance on heavy weld-out jobs.
  • Bought-ins: bolts, anchors, mesh, grating, handrail fittings — get supplier quotes; don’t guess.
  • Waste and offcuts: if you can’t use the drop on this job, someone pays for it — usually you, unless the quote allowed for it.

Travel, site allowance, cranage, NDT, coatings

List these as their own lines so the client sees them and you don’t bury them in a cheap lump sum:

  • Travel / mobilisation (km or hours portal-to-portal — be consistent)
  • Site or living-away allowances where the job requires them (check Fair Work / award if employing)
  • Cranage, EWP, scaffolding or traffic control you have to hire
  • NDT (visual is often in-house; MPI/UT/RT are usually specialist quotes)
  • Surface treatment (galv, paint system, blast) — get the galvaniser or painter’s price before you tender if the spec is fixed
  • Permits, hot-work tickets, SWMS time on high-risk site work

Contingency and variation risk

Contingency is a risk allowance, not profit. Put it on the build-up (often ~5–15% of direct cost for clear shop work; higher when drawings are incomplete, site access is unknown, or coatings/NDT are “TBA”). When the client changes scope, don’t silently eat contingency — raise a variation with a written instruction, then claim it on the next progress claim (see progress claims, variations and retention).

Quote presentation and GST

A commercial quote that wins and gets paid usually shows:

  • Job description, drawings/revision, inclusions and exclusions
  • Breakdown or schedule of rates the client can certify against
  • Validity period, deposit or progress terms, lead time
  • GST treatment in writing

If you are GST-registered (or required to be), taxable sales generally include GST in the price: you charge GST, issue a tax invoice, and remit it on your BAS — see the ATO pages on taxable sales and tax invoices. Commercial clients usually prefer ex-GST figures with GST shown separately. If you are not registered, do not charge GST and do not label the document a tax invoice. Worked example (made-up numbers — example only)

Small QLD fab shop quoting a galvanised access platform. Charge-out used in this example: A$95/hour ex GST (your real rate will differ — calculate it).

  • Labour: 38 hours (cut/fit/weld/grind/QA/pack) × A$95 = A$3,610
  • Steel (nested stock lengths + plate, after supplier quote) = A$2,400
  • Consumables allowance = A$180
  • Galvanising (supplier quote) = A$920
  • Delivery / travel = A$220
  • Subtotal direct = A$7,330
  • Contingency 10% = A$733
  • Quote ex GST = A$8,063
  • If GST-registered: GST 10% = A$806.30 → total inc GST A$8,869.30

After the job, compare actual hours and steel purchases to this build-up. That feedback loop is what the job-costing kit is for — without it, the next platform quote repeats the same under-estimate.

Common questions

Should I quote welding jobs ex GST or including GST?

If you are GST-registered (or required to be), commercial clients usually want an ex-GST price with GST shown separately so they can claim credits. State clearly on the quote whether figures are ex GST or include GST. If you are not registered, do not charge GST and do not call the document a tax invoice — see the ATO tax invoice rules.

What charge-out rate should a small fab shop use?

Build it from your real wage or drawings, super (if you employ), workshop overhead, unbillable hours and a profit margin — then check it against similar shops. Use the free charge-out calculator on this site. Labour cost inputs for employees must meet Fair Work / modern award minimums; do not invent award rates — look up the applicable award on Fair Work.

How much contingency should I put on a fabrication quote?

Many small shops use roughly 5–15% of direct cost for shop work with clear drawings, and more when drawings are incomplete, site access is unknown, or coatings/NDT are unspecified. Contingency is not profit — keep it as its own line so you can see when variations eat it.

Why do my welding jobs make less than the quote?

Usually under-estimated labour (fit-up, grind, rework, waiting on site), steel ordered as total metres instead of nested stock lengths, forgotten consumables and coatings, or travel/cranage left out. Compare quoted vs actual on every job so the next quote is honest.
