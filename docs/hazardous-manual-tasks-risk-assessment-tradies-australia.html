Hazardous manual tasks: how tradies assess and control lifting risks

Last reviewed 21 November 2026 · Sources linked below · Written by Jay at Andwat Co, an Australian boilermaker/fabricator (Cert III Engineering, 12+ years in the trade). About

What makes a manual task hazardous under the WHS rules, WorkSafe Queensland's four risk questions (repetition, duration, force, vibration), common fab shop and site examples, and controls that work.

Lifting steel, carrying sheet, holding a grinder overhead for an hour, tying reo bent over all day: these are hazardous manual tasks, and they cause more workers' compensation claims than almost anything else in the trades. The law doesn't hand you a single "safe" weight. Instead it asks you to look at the forces, postures, movements, duration and vibration involved. Here's how to do that in a small business.

The short answer

  • A manual task is hazardous when it involves repetitive or sustained force, high or sudden force, repetitive movement, sustained or awkward posture, or exposure to vibration.
  • The PCBU must manage the risk of musculoskeletal disorders (MSDs) from hazardous manual tasks under Part 4.2 of the WHS Regulation. In Queensland the Hazardous manual tasks Code of Practice 2021 sets out how.
  • Do a risk assessment for each hazardous manual task unless the risk is already well known and you know how to control it.
  • Fix it with the hierarchy of control: get rid of the task, change the workplace or task design, use mechanical aids, and only then rely on training and procedures.

The four risk questions (WorkSafe Queensland)

QuestionGeneral guideline
1. Repetitive or sustained movement, posture or force?Repetitive = more than twice a minute. Sustained = held for more than 30 seconds at a time.
2. Long duration?More than 2 hours in total over a shift, or more than 30 minutes continuously.
3. High or sudden force?The worker says it's physically demanding, needs help or a second person, or a one-handed job needs two hands. Jerky or unexpected loads count as sudden force.
4. Vibration?Whole-body (plant seats) or hand-arm (grinders, needle scalers, rattle guns), with risk rising with exposure time.

If the answer to 1 and 2 is yes, or 3 is yes, there's a risk to control. Talk to the people doing the job first: aches and pains, near misses and past claims point straight at the worst tasks.

Common hazardous manual tasks in a fab shop or on site

  • Loading and unloading lengths of RHS, angle and flat bar by hand.
  • Flipping heavy fabrications on the bench to weld the other side.
  • Overhead grinding or welding for long periods.
  • Carrying sheet, plate or gates up stairs or across rough ground.
  • Kneeling or bending to tie reo, lay pavers or fix bottom plates.
  • Repetitive drilling, tapping or deburring at a badly set bench height.

Controls that actually work

  1. Eliminate: have steel delivered cut-to-length and to the point of use; fabricate at ground level instead of in place.
  2. Change the design of the task or workplace: adjustable bench heights, weld positioners and rotators, racks at waist height, smaller packs from the supplier.
  3. Mechanical aids: gantry or jib crane, forklift, lifting magnets, plate clamps, trolleys and sheet carts, vacuum lifters. Lifting gear needs its own inspections.
  4. Administrative: job rotation, breaks, two-person lifts for awkward loads, and training on the aids you've provided. These support the higher controls, they don't replace them.

Write it down

For each task, record what the task is, which risk factors you found, the controls chosen, who's responsible and a review date. FAQ

Is there a maximum weight a worker can lift in Australia?

The WHS laws don't set a single legal weight limit. You assess the forces, postures, movements, duration and vibration of each task and control the risk, as set out in the Hazardous manual tasks Code of Practice.

When is a manual task hazardous?

When it involves repetitive or sustained force, high or sudden force, repetitive movement, sustained or awkward posture, or exposure to vibration.

Do I need a written manual handling risk assessment?

You should carry out a risk assessment for each hazardous manual task unless the risk is well known and you already know how to control it. Writing it down helps show what you did and when to review it.

What counts as repetitive or long duration?

As a general guideline from WorkSafe Queensland, repetitive means more than twice a minute, sustained means held for more than 30 seconds, and long duration means more than 2 hours over a shift or more than 30 minutes continuously.
