Steel weight per metre: the formulas

Last reviewed 25 October 2026 · Sources linked below · Written by Jay at Andwat Co, an Australian boilermaker/fabricator (Cert III Engineering, 12+ years in the trade). About

How to work out steel weight in kg/m from section dimensions using 7,850 kg/m³: flat bar, round bar, plate, SHS/RHS and CHS, with worked examples.

Carbon steel catalogues in Australia, such as InfraBuild's Know Your Steel, work out mass using a density of 7,850 kg/m³. That means 1 mm² of cross-section weighs 0.00785 kg per metre. Work out the cross-sectional area in mm², multiply by 0.00785, and you have kg/m.

Formulas (dimensions in mm)

Sectionkg/m
Flat bar W × TW × T × 0.00785
Round bar, diameter Dπ × D² ÷ 4 × 0.00785
Plate, thickness TT × 7.85 kg per m²
SHS/RHS B × D × T (square corners, approx)(2 × (B + D) − 4 × T) × T × 0.00785
CHS, outside diameter OD × wall Tπ × (OD − T) × T × 0.00785

Worked examples

  • 50 × 6 flat bar: 50 × 6 × 0.00785 = 2.355 kg/m
  • 20 mm round bar: π × 400 ÷ 4 × 0.00785 ≈ 2.466 kg/m
  • 10 mm plate: 10 × 7.85 = 78.5 kg/m², so a 1.2 × 2.4 m sheet is about 226 kg
  • 50 × 50 × 3 SHS: (200 − 12) × 3 × 0.00785 ≈ 4.43 kg/m (square-corner estimate)

Use the supplier's tables for real sections

Hollow sections have rounded corners, so the square-corner formula reads a little high. Rolled sections (UB, UC, PFC, angles) have published kg/m values. For quoting and ordering, use the supplier's catalogue figure and keep the formula for flat bar, plate and odd sizes. Catalogues also note rolling tolerances, so weighbridge weights can differ a little from calculated ones.

From kg to a price

Total kg = kg/m × length (m) × quantity. If your supplier prices by the kilo, multiply by the $/kg; if they price by the length, use that instead. Add an allowance for offcuts.
