Ladder safety rules for construction work in QLD: angle, length, 120 kg and tie-off

Last reviewed 28 November 2026 · Sources linked below · Written by Jay at Andwat Co, an Australian boilermaker/fabricator (Cert III Engineering, 12+ years in the trade). About

The QLD ladder rules for construction work from WHS Regulation ss 306L and 306M and the falls code of practice: 120 kg industrial rating, length limits, 70-80 degree angle, 1 m above the landing, securing, permitted work, and no welding from a ladder.

The ladder is the first thing most of us grab and, according to WorkSafe Queensland's falls code of practice, it should be one of the last: falls from ladders have caused serious and fatal injuries even from low heights. Here's what the QLD Work Health and Safety Regulation 2011 (ss 306L and 306M) and the code say, including one rule every welder should know: the code says it's not safe to arc weld or oxy cut from a ladder.

The short answer

  • Ladder rated 120 kg and made for industrial use (s 306M(1)). No domestic or homemade ladders on site.
  • Length limits: single ladder no longer than 6.1 m, extension ladder no longer than 7.5 m (9.2 m if used for electrical work) (s 306M(1)(e)). These limits don't apply to work in a confined space.
  • Angle 70° to 80° for single and extension ladders, and at least 1 m above the surface you're stepping onto when it's used for access (s 306M(8)). The code's rule of thumb is 4:1: about 1 m out at the base for every 4 m of working height.
  • Stable base, rungs about level (s 306M(6)), and never support a platform off a ladder (s 306M(7)). Ladder platform brackets must not be used for construction work in Queensland.
  • 2 m (3 m for housing) or more? On a single or extension ladder you need 3 limbs on the ladder (or a pole strap, or a harness not attached to the ladder) and the ladder secured at or near the top or bottom (s 306L).
  • Not for hot work. The falls code lists arc welding and oxy cutting from a ladder as not safe, along with metal or metal-reinforced ladders on live electrical installations.

When a ladder is the right tool

The Managing the risk of falls at workplaces Code of Practice 2021 says ladders should only be considered after safer options like an EWP or scaffold have been considered and found not reasonably practicable. Single and extension ladders should only be used to get on and off a work area, or for short-duration light work that can be done safely from the ladder. A step platform, with its small working platform and partial handrail, gives better fall protection than a traditional stepladder for many jobs.

"Permitted work" on a ladder (s 306L)

For construction work where someone could fall 2 m or more (3 m for housing construction work), the regulation only allows a single or extension ladder for permitted work. That means: what you're carrying won't restrict your movement or make you lose balance, your trunk stays roughly centred between the stiles, and any equipment can be run with one hand (unless you're wearing something like a pole strap that supports your body). If the job needs a two-handed tool, a big sheet or a heavy part, it's not ladder work. Get an EWP, scaffold or mobile platform.

Set-up checklist

RatingMarked 120 kg (or more) and industrial use by the manufacturer.
ConditionInspected before use: no broken rungs, stiles or feet, locks working. Faulty or damaged ladders removed from service.
HeightTall enough that you don't over-reach. Don't stand on a rung closer than 900 mm to the top of a single or extension ladder.
BaseSolid, stable surface, non-slip feet, rungs about level. Not resting the weight on the rungs.

SecuredTied or clamped at the top or bottom, or footed by a second person (the regulation's own example is clamping the top to structural steel).

SurroundingsDoorways barricaded or locked, traffic kept out, no work in very wet or windy conditions, and no ladder next to powerlines unless the worker is trained and authorised and using the right ladder. See the overhead power lines exclusion zones guide.

On the ladder

  • Three points of contact: two feet and a hand climbing, or two feet and a hand or thighs against the ladder while working.
  • Tools in a belt or pouch, not in your hands while climbing. Light work only, with tools you can run one-handed.
  • Face the ladder. Don't straddle it. One person on it at a time and nobody working underneath.
  • No two-handed tools (circular saws, concrete saws) and no high-leverage tools like stillsons or pinch bars that can throw you off balance.
  • Slip-resistant footwear.

Fibreglass ladders: check for damage and UV

A WorkSafe Queensland alert describes a worker seriously injured in March 2020 when a 120 kg-rated fibreglass dual-purpose ladder, which appeared to be several years old, snapped at the main hinge and he fell about 2 m. The alert says to inspect ladders when first put into service, before each use, after drops and impacts, periodically, and for UV degradation. Look for cracking or separating fibreglass (especially around rivets and bolt holes), "blooming" where glass fibres break through the surface, loose rungs or hinges, missing or worn feet and non-genuine repairs. Defective ladders get marked and taken out of service for repair by a competent person (the manufacturer or its agent) or destroyed.

What to keep on file

For a small crew that usually means a toolbox talk on ladders with signed attendance, the ladders listed with their inspection dates, and a defect log showing damaged ladders were tagged out. If the work is 2 m or more on a construction site, it's high risk construction work and needs a SWMS. See the 2 m and 3 m fall rules guide.

FAQ

What angle should a ladder be at in Queensland?

For construction work, a single or extension ladder must be placed between 70 and 80 degrees to the horizontal when in use (QLD WHS Regulation s 306M(8)). The falls code of practice's rule of thumb is a 4:1 slope: about 1 metre out at the base for every 4 metres of working ladder height.

What is the maximum length of an extension ladder in QLD?

For construction work, an extension ladder must be no longer than 7.5 m, or 9.2 m if it's used for electrical work, and a single ladder no longer than 6.1 m (s 306M(1)(e)). The limits don't apply to ladders used in a confined space.

Can you weld from a ladder?

WorkSafe Queensland's Managing the risk of falls at workplaces Code of Practice 2021 lists carrying out hot work like arc welding or oxy cutting from a ladder as not safe. Use an EWP, scaffold or mobile platform and plan the work as hot work.

Do I need to tie off a ladder?

For construction work where a person could fall 2 m or more (3 m for housing), a single or extension ladder must be secured at or near the top or bottom to stop it moving, for example clamped to structural steel or footed by a second person, as well as the user keeping 3 limbs on the ladder or using a pole strap or a harness not attached to the ladder (s 306L).
