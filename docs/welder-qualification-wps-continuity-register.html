Welder qualification register and WPS continuity log (AU)

Last reviewed 29 October 2026 · Sources linked below · Written by Jay at Andwat Co, an Australian boilermaker/fabricator (Cert III Engineering, 12+ years in the trade). About

What Australian fab shops usually keep in a welder qualification register and WPS list: the difference between a WPS, a PQR and a welder ticket, 6-month confirmation and continuity evidence, and the fields clients ask for at audit.

On structural steel jobs, clients don't just ask "are your welders ticketed?". They ask for a set of records: which welding procedure specification (WPS) covers each joint, how that procedure was qualified, which named welder is qualified to run it, and evidence that the qualification is still current. A register that answers those questions in one place saves the scramble at handover. This guide is about that paperwork. It doesn't reproduce Australian Standards (they're copyright), and it isn't engineering advice — buy the current editions your contract calls up and read the job specification.

For the Standards landscape (AS/NZS 1554, AS/NZS 2980, AS/NZS ISO 9606-1 and CC3 under AS/NZS 5131), see our welding tickets guide and the companion WPS vs PQR guide.

Three different things people mix up

The Australian Welding Institute puts it plainly: procedure qualification and welder qualification answer two different questions.

  • WPS (welding procedure specification) — the instruction the welder follows (process, material, consumable, preheat, positions, thickness range and so on).
  • PQR / WPQR (procedure qualification record) — the tested evidence that the method can produce an acceptable weld within a stated range. Under AS/NZS 1554.1, many routine structural joints can sit inside prequalified limits, but you still need a written WPS that shows the joint is inside those limits.
  • Welder qualification (the "ticket") — evidence that a named person produced an acceptable test weld for a defined range of process, material, joint type, thickness and position.

AWI's checklist before anyone strikes an arc: every production joint must sit inside both the procedure range and the welder's range. Holding one document tells you nothing about the other.

What usually sits in a WPS register

  • WPS number and revision
  • how it was qualified (prequalified, prior experience, or by test) and the PQR reference if any
  • process, parent material, thickness and positions
  • consumable, shielding gas and preheat / interpass notes
  • approval status, and which welders are currently qualified to it

Gaps that fail audits are missing approvals, WPSs with no current welder, and procedures whose range doesn't cover the joints on the drawing.

What usually sits in a welder qualification register

  • welder name and ID
  • Standard and edition (for example AS/NZS 1554.1, AS/NZS 2980, AS/NZS ISO 9606-1, AS/NZS 3992)
  • certificate or test reference, process, WPS link, positions, thickness / diameter range and test date
  • last verified production use, continuity / confirmation due date, revalidation or hard expiry if any, and a clear status (current, due soon, lapsed)

Match each welder's range to the work they'll actually do. A plate fillet ticket doesn't cover pipe in position, and a butt-weld qualification may not cover fillets under newer routes — check the certificate and the Standard, not tribal memory.

Continuity and 6-month confirmation

Under the AS/NZS 2980 / ISO 9606-style routes used with WeldQ and discussed at ASI's welder-qualification forum, qualifications typically need confirmation every 6 months that the welder has continued to weld successfully within the certificate limits. Weld Australia's supervisor guidance describes confirmation as the welding supervisor or coordinator verifying successful production welding in the previous six months, and notes the qualification can go on hold if confirmation isn't completed by the due date. The Standards also set longer revalidation options; the details sit in the paid Standards, so ask your testing body which option each certificate uses.

A continuity log is the shop-floor evidence behind those confirmations: each checked production weld, with job, WPS, result, report reference and who verified it. Only acceptable, verified welds keep a qualification current. Diarise confirmation and revalidation dates the same way you diarise White Cards and crane tickets.

A welder × WPS matrix

For day-to-day allocation, a simple matrix (welder down the side, WPS across the top) showing OK / due / lapsed / check is what supervisors reach for. It doesn't replace the certificates or the WPSs — it points at them.

Practical checklist

  1. Read the specification: which Standard, which construction category, any client-specific welder rules.
  2. List every WPS you'll use and the qualification path behind it.
  3. List each welder's current tickets and match ranges to the joints.
  4. Keep a continuity log that feeds the 6-month confirmations.
  5. File the register with the ITP, weld maps and NDT records the builder will ask for at handover.
