Working at heights in QLD: the 2 m and 3 m fall protection rules

Last reviewed 21 November 2026 · Sources linked below · Written by Jay at Andwat Co, an Australian boilermaker/fabricator (Cert III Engineering, 12+ years in the trade). About

Queensland's fall rules in plain English: when a SWMS is needed (over 2 m), when sections 306C and 306D apply (2 m non-housing, 3 m housing, roofs over 26°), which controls count, and a simple pre-start routine.

Falls from height are one of the main ways tradies get killed or badly hurt on Queensland sites. Queensland's rules have two numbers every builder, roofer, fabricator and installer should know: 2 metres and 3 metres. Here's what each one triggers, in plain English, with the official sources linked below.

The short answer

  • Every workplace: a PCBU must manage the risk of a fall from one level to another that is reasonably likely to cause injury (WHS Regulation s78). First choice is doing the work on the ground or on a solid construction, with safe access and exit.
  • More than 2 m in construction work: it's high risk construction work, so a SWMS is required. This applies to housing construction too.
  • At least 2 m in non-housing construction, at least 3 m in housing construction, or a roof slope over 26°: section 306D applies. You must use controls that prevent a fall where reasonably practicable (e.g. edge protection), otherwise controls that arrest the fall and minimise injury (e.g. a catch platform). Training, signs and PPE on their own are not enough.
  • Below those heights: section 306C still applies. Before work starts you must identify the fall hazards, assess the risk and use the controls needed. "It's only 1.8 m" isn't a free pass.

2 m vs 3 m at a glance (QLD construction work)

SituationWhat applies
Housing construction, fall risk under 3 ms306C: identify hazards, assess risk, use controls before work starts. SWMS required if the fall risk is more than 2 m.
Housing construction, fall risk 3 m or mores306D: fall prevention (or fall arrest if prevention isn't practicable) must be in place. SWMS required.
Other construction (commercial, industrial, civil), under 2 ms306C: identify hazards, assess risk, use controls.
Other construction, 2 m or mores306D: prevention or arrest controls required. SWMS required for more than 2 m.
Any construction work on a roof sloped over 26°s306D, regardless of height.

WorkSafe Queensland measures the 3 m housing fall risk from the lowest edge of the roof or work area you're on. Section 306C and 306D don't apply to a fall from a ladder, a trestle-ladder platform, scaffolding you are erecting or dismantling, or the area next to a ladder you're using to get on or off. Those are managed under the general rules and the code of practice.

What counts as a control (s306E to s306J)

  1. Edge protection (guardrails, scaffold edge) on the open edge.
  2. Fall protection covers over penetrations and openings, secured and marked.
  3. Travel restraint: a harness and line set so you can't reach the edge.
  4. Fall-arresting platforms (catch platforms) below the work.
  5. Fall-arrest harness systems with proper anchors, a rescue plan and trained users.
  6. Safety nets.

Each one has its own technical requirements in the Regulation. If you rely on administrative controls (training, no-go zones, a spotter) for falls over 2 m, the SWMS has to explain why higher-order controls weren't used.

A simple pre-start routine for height work

  1. Can it be done on the ground? Fabricate and pre-assemble at ground level or in the shop, then lift into place.
  2. Measure the fall. Is it housing or other construction? Is the roof over 26°? That decides 306C vs 306D.
  3. Pick the highest practical control: scaffold or edge protection first, then EWP, then restraint, then arrest.
  4. Write or review the SWMS if the fall risk is more than 2 m, and make sure the crew has read it.
  5. Check the gear: scaffold tagged and inspected, harness and lanyard inspected, anchors rated, rescue plan written down.
  6. Brief the crew with a toolbox talk before you start, and record who attended.

FAQ

Is there a 2 metre rule for working at heights in Queensland?

Yes. Construction work with a risk of a fall of more than 2 metres is high risk construction work, so a SWMS is required. In non-housing construction, a fall risk of 2 metres or more also means fall prevention or fall arrest controls must be used under section 306D of the WHS Regulation.

What is the 3 metre rule in Queensland housing construction?

In housing construction, where a person could fall 3 metres or more (or the roof slope is over 26°), section 306D requires fall prevention controls such as edge protection, or fall arrest controls if prevention isn't reasonably practicable. Administrative controls on their own are not permitted.

Do I need fall protection under 2 metres?

Possibly. Section 306C requires you to identify fall hazards, assess the risk and use any controls needed before work starts, even below the 2 m or 3 m trigger.

Do the 306C and 306D rules apply to ladders?

No. They don't apply to a fall from a ladder, a platform on trestle ladders, scaffolding you are erecting or dismantling, or the area near a ladder you're getting on or off. Ladder work is still managed under the general fall rules and the code of practice.
