How a charge-out rate is worked out

A common mistake is to take the wage you want and divide it by the hours you work. That leaves out three things that a business has to pay for: super, overheads, and all the hours nobody pays you for. The calculator works it out in four steps:

  1. Add up a year of costs. That's the wage you'd want as an employee, super on top if you want it (the ATO's super guarantee rate for employees is 12%), and your overheads, such as the ute, fuel, insurance, tools, phone, software and the accountant. Leave out materials you charge to jobs.
  2. Work out your billable hours for a year. Start with 52 weeks, take off holidays, public holidays, and sick, rain and slow days, then multiply by the hours a week that actually end up on an invoice. Quoting, driving, picking up gear and paperwork are work, but you usually can't bill them.
  3. Break-even rate = yearly costs ÷ billable hours. Charge less than this and you're paying to go to work.
  4. Add profit as a margin. Divide the break-even rate by (1 − margin), not times (1 + margin). A 15% margin means 15% of the price is profit, which is a markup of about 17.6%.

Example: $120,800 a year of wage, super and overheads, spread over 1,320 billable hours (30 a week for 44 weeks), gives a break-even of $91.52 an hour. Dividing by 0.85 for a 15% margin gives a charge-out rate of $107.66 an hour.

GST: registered or not

  • Registered for GST: enter your overheads without GST, because you claim the GST back. The rate the calculator shows is ex GST, so add 10% when you quote prices that include GST.
  • Not registered: leave the box unticked. Enter what you actually pay, including GST, because you can't claim it back, and don't add GST to your rate. If the sales you need come to $75,000 a year or more, you'd have to register, and the calculator warns you. See Do I need to register for GST?
