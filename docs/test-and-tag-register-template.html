What to put in an Australian test and tag register: asset ID, test date, next due, outcome, tester, intervals by environment, and a printable row layout that links to ready spreadsheets.

Auditors and principal contractors ask for a test and tag register, not a box of loose tags. Our test and tag guide covers AS/NZS 3760 intervals. This page is the register template: the columns that stop overdue leads staying on site.

The short answer

  • One row per asset: lead, tool, RCD, generator outlet, temporary board.
  • Record asset ID, description, location, test date, next due, result (pass/fail), tester name and licence/competency, and comments.
  • Set the interval from the environment (construction, factory, office, hire) per AS/NZS 3760 guidance – do not invent a single "yearly" date for everything.
  • Failed items are quarantined and tagged out until repaired and retested.
  • Colour overdue next-due dates so they jump off the sheet.

Worked example – mobile welder kit

Twelve leads and tools on the ute. Construction environment → 3-month interval for Class I equipment. Register sorted by next due. Anything inside 14 days of due gets tested on the next quiet Friday. Failed grinder lead quarantined in a red tub, not left coiled with the good leads.

Useful gear (affiliate): digital multimeter for basic continuity checks by a competent person, and a LED torch when tagging boards in dark switch rooms. Testing itself must be done by a competent person with the right equipment.

Quick answers

What interval should my register use?

Follow AS/NZS 3760 guidance for the environment and equipment class, plus manufacturer instructions. Construction sites are typically shorter than offices.

Can I keep tags without a register?

Tags help on the tool. The register is what proves history, next due and who tested it.

Who can test and tag?

A competent person. Some states have extra licensing rules for certain work. Check your state regulator.

Should plant inspections share the same due date?

No. Plant, lifting gear and electrical test-and-tag are different systems. One workbook with separate due columns is fine.
