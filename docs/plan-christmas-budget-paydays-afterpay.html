Plan an Australian Christmas budget around paydays and Afterpay

Last reviewed 14 November 2026 · Sources linked below · Written by Jay at Andwat Co, an Australian boilermaker/fabricator (Cert III Engineering, 12+ years in the trade). About

Work out what Christmas will cost, how much to set aside each payday, and which Afterpay payments land in January. A$9 AU planner.

By mid-November the gifts, the lunch and the pay-in-4 payments are already stacking up. From 14 November 2026 there are 41 days until Christmas Day on Thursday 25 December 2026. If you read this later, recount the days from today's date. The useful trick is to separate two questions: what Christmas will cost, and when the money actually leaves your account.

This is a practical planning guide for Australian households, not financial advice, and not a sales pitch dressed up as a countdown. There is no single “right” Christmas budget. The aim is a clear list and a payday plan you can keep to.

The short answer

  • Count the whole cost, not just the presents. Christmas Eve, Christmas Day lunch, Boxing Day, New Year's Eve, decorations, cards, postage, wrapping and travel often sit off the gift list and still come out of the same weeks of pay.
  • Plan against your payday, weekly or fortnightly. Count the paydays left on your own cycle until 25 December, then divide what's still to cover by that number.
  • Afterpay is four payments, not one. Afterpay's Australian how-it-works page says Pay in 4 is four payments over six weeks: about 25% at purchase, then the rest approximately every two weeks. Instalments after 25 December land in January.
  • Use the dates in your provider's app. A spreadsheet cannot log into Afterpay, Zip or similar, and should not be treated as a bank feed.
  • Keep the gift list moving: one row per person, and tick bought, wrapped and given so you can see who is still waiting.

Count the whole cost, not just the presents

The gift list is usually the part people start. It is rarely the whole bill. Food and events can quietly match or beat the presents: the ham, the salads, Christmas Eve drinks, Boxing Day leftovers you still shop for, New Year's Eve, decorations, cards, postage, wrapping and any travel to family.

Make one list with a budget and an actual for each line. When a gift or a shop costs more than you planned, write the actual down rather than hoping it will even out. A simple status helps on the presents: to buy, to wrap, to give, then done. If you group people — family, kids, partner, friends, Kris Kringle, teachers — you can see which group is soaking the budget.

Plan against your payday, weekly or fortnightly

If you are paid fortnightly, you have only a few paydays left before Christmas. How many is your number, not a number from this page. Count the pay dates that still fall before 25 December on your own cycle.

A useful figure is:

Amount still to cover ÷ paydays left = amount to set aside each payday.

“Still to cover” is your Christmas budget minus what you have already set aside or already spent. If you get paid weekly, the same method works with a larger number of paydays and a smaller amount each time. The planner's summary counts days to Christmas, paydays left (weekly or fortnightly) and the per-payday figure on one A4 page, but you can do the arithmetic in any notebook.

Afterpay is four payments, not one

Pay-in-4 is easy to treat as a single checkout total. It is not. Afterpay's Australian how Pay in 4 works page says you get the purchase now and pay for it in four payments over six weeks, interest-free. Afterpay pays the merchant in full. You make the first payment (approximately 25% of the total) at the time of purchase, and the remaining three payments are automatically deducted from your preferred payment method approximately every two weeks.

Other pay-later providers use their own schedules. Some purchases have no payment upfront, so the first date is later than checkout. Always use the dates in the provider's app. A spreadsheet can list the four dates, what is still owing, what is due before Christmas and what lands in January, and flag amounts that are due soon or overdue, but it does not link to your bank or BNPL account and it does not send reminders. You can type over any date if the app shows something different.

The January figure is the instalments that fall after 25 December. That is the part that surprises people in the new year. Add it up before you hit confirm on another pay-in-4 purchase, not after.

Keep the gift list moving

One row per person is enough: name, group, idea, budget and actual. Tick bought, wrapped and given so the status can move from to buy, to wrap, to give, then done. Questions people ask

How do I work out how much to put aside each payday before Christmas?

Count the paydays left on your own cycle, weekly or fortnightly, from today until Christmas Day (25 December). Then divide the amount you still need to cover — your budget minus what you have already set aside or spent — by that number of paydays. The result is the amount to set aside each payday. Do not use someone else's payday count; it depends on when you are paid.

When do Afterpay Pay in 4 payments come out?

Afterpay's Australian how-it-works page says Pay in 4 is four payments over six weeks, interest-free. The first payment is approximately 25% of the total at the time of purchase, and the remaining three payments are automatically deducted approximately every two weeks. Some purchases have no payment upfront; use the dates in your provider's app, because a spreadsheet cannot log into Afterpay for you. Instalments that fall after 25 December are the ones that land in January.
