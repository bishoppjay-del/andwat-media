Stocktake & ATO trading stock rules for tradies and small shops

Last reviewed 26 November 2026 · Sources linked below · Written by Jay at Andwat Co, an Australian boilermaker/fabricator (Cert III Engineering, 12+ years in the trade). About

Not every tradie or small seller has to count every bolt and shelf item on 30 June. Under the ATO's simplified trading stock rules, if your stock value changed by $5,000 or less over the year, you may be able to use a reasonable estimate instead — here is how the rule works.

What counts as trading stock

The ATO defines trading stock as anything your business buys, makes or manufactures to sell, manufacture or exchange — including raw materials, spare parts or finished goods you hold for sale. Tools and equipment you use to do the work (not to sell) are depreciating assets, not trading stock.

The $5,000 simplified trading stock rule

You can use the ATO's simplified trading stock rules if your business has an aggregated turnover under $50 million (either a small business under $10 million, or $10 million to under $50 million for income years starting on or after 1 July 2021) and you reasonably estimate that the total value of your trading stock changed by $5,000 or less between the start and end of the income year.

If you meet both tests and choose the simplified rules:

  • You do not have to conduct a formal year-end stocktake.
  • You do not have to account for the change in your trading stock's value in your tax return (your closing stock value is treated as the same as your opening value).
  • You still claim deductions for stock purchases in the same way you claim your other business expenses.

ATO example (electrician with van and workshop stock): Colin holds a steady mix of parts in his van and workshop. Last 30 June his trading stock was $6,800. Quantities haven't shifted much, and supplier prices rose around 15% ($6,800 × 1.15 = $7,820). The change is $1,020 — under $5,000 — so Colin doesn't need to run a formal stocktake for tax.

When you must do a stocktake (general rules)

If your reasonable estimate shows your trading stock value rose or fell by more than $5,000 during the year (or if your aggregated turnover is $50 million or more, or you choose to account for the change), you must use the general trading stock rules under section 70-35 of the ITAA 1997:

  • Count and value all trading stock on hand at 30 June — including stock you have received even if you haven't paid the supplier invoice yet.
  • If closing stock is higher than opening stock, the increase is included in your assessable income. If closing stock is lower, the decrease is an allowable deduction.
  • Value each item using one of three ATO methods: cost price (code C), market selling value (code M), or replacement value (code R). You can choose a different method for different items or switch from year to year.

How to run a clean count without double-counting

Whether you're doing an EOFY stocktake for your accountant or checking shelf shrinkage mid-year, most count blowouts happen from messy process rather than maths:

  • Freeze or log movements: ideally count outside trading hours. If stock is still arriving or shipping during the count, log every receipt and dispatch on a movements sheet (counted qty + IN − OUT = adjusted qty).
  • Check units vs cartons: verify whether a SKU is tracked per box, per length or per piece before counting.
  • Quarantine damaged or reserved stock: separate stock already sold/invoiced and waiting pickup so it isn't counted twice, and note damaged or obsolete items separately.
  • Recount variances before adjusting: when physical count differs from your system, recount that bin and check unrecorded handovers or open invoices before writing it off.

Keep your count sheets, valuation notes and reasoning for your estimate with your business records — see our guide on how long to keep business records (the ATO 5-year rule).

Common questions

What is the ATO $5,000 trading stock threshold?

If your aggregated turnover is under $50 million and you reasonably estimate that the value of your trading stock changed by $5,000 or less over the income year, the ATO simplified trading stock rules let you skip a formal stocktake and treat closing stock as equal to opening stock.

How do I have to value trading stock for the ATO?

Under the general trading stock rules, you value each item at 30 June using cost price, market selling value, or replacement value. You can choose the method per item and change methods between income years.

Do I still have to prove my $5,000 estimate if I don't do a stocktake?

Yes. The ATO requires your estimate to be made in good faith following a rational process based on your records, and you must be able to explain and substantiate how you arrived at it if asked.
