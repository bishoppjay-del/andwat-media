Asbestos removal in QLD: the 10 m² rule and Class A vs B licences

Last reviewed 22 November 2026 · Sources linked below · Written by Jay at Andwat Co, an Australian boilermaker/fabricator (Cert III Engineering, 12+ years in the trade). About

When you need a licensed removalist in Queensland (any friable, or more than 10 m² bonded), what the no-licence 10 m² job still requires, banned tools (s446) and which workplaces need an asbestos register.

Plenty of Queensland houses, sheds and workshops built before 1990 have fibro (asbestos cement sheeting) in walls, eaves, roofs, fences and wet-area backing boards. If you're a tradie cutting into, drilling, fixing to or pulling out old sheeting, you need to know the 10 square metre rule and the tools you're not allowed to use. Here's how it works, straight from WorkSafe Queensland and the WHS Regulation.

The short answer

  • Friable asbestos (crumbles to powder by hand pressure when dry): any amount needs a Class A licence holder.
  • Non-friable (bonded) asbestos, more than 10 m²: needs a Class B (or Class A) licence holder (WHS Regulation s487).
  • Non-friable, 10 m² or less: no licence needed, but the work must still follow Part 8.7 of the WHS Regulation and the How to safely remove asbestos Code of Practice 2021. 10 m² is about the size of a small bathroom.
  • Banned on asbestos, full stop: high-pressure water spray and compressed air (s446).
  • Banned unless controlled: power tools, brooms and anything else that releases fibres, unless the tool is enclosed or designed and used to capture or suppress the dust.

Asbestos removal licences are only issued to a business (PCBU) and last five years. You can check whether a removalist holds a Class A or Class B licence using the registers on WorkSafe Queensland's website. Homeowners removing more than 10 m² of non-friable asbestos at home need a certificate under arrangements approved by Queensland Health.

If you're doing the small job yourself (10 m² or less)

The Code of Practice lists what a PCBU without a licence must still do:

  1. Get a copy of the asbestos register for the workplace (not required for residential premises).
  2. Identify the hazards and put up signs and barricades around the work area.
  3. Use the wet method where reasonably practicable.
  4. Use the right tools, equipment and PPE (hand tools, HEPA-filtered H-class vacuums, not a household vac).
  5. Have decontamination facilities available.
  6. Contain and label asbestos waste and dispose of it at a site licensed to accept asbestos waste as soon as reasonably practicable.
  7. Make sure workers are trained in identifying and safely handling asbestos first. The Code says an asbestos awareness course or the non-friable removal unit would be appropriate training.

Asbestos registers: which workplaces need one

In Queensland, the person with management or control of a workplace must identify asbestos, record it in an asbestos register, and have a written asbestos management plan if asbestos is identified or likely. A register isn't required only if the building was constructed after 31 December 1989, no asbestos has been identified and none is likely. Registers must be kept up to date, be readily accessible, and be reviewed at least every five years. Before demolition, refurbishment, maintenance or service work, the register must be reviewed and a copy given to the contractor doing the work.

For a fab shop or contractor that means: ask for the register before you drill, cut or weld on an older building or old plant. Plant installed before 31 December 2003 can also contain asbestos (for example gaskets).

FAQ

Can I remove asbestos without a licence in Queensland?

Only 10 square metres or less of non-friable (bonded) asbestos, plus minor associated dust or debris. The work must still follow Part 8.7 of the WHS Regulation and the How to safely remove asbestos Code of Practice 2021. Any friable asbestos needs a Class A licence.

What is the difference between a Class A and Class B asbestos licence?

Class A covers any amount of friable asbestos and also non-friable asbestos. Class B covers removal of more than 10 square metres of non-friable (bonded) asbestos and the associated dust or debris, but not friable asbestos.

Can I use an angle grinder or circular saw on fibro?

Not unless the tool is controlled, meaning it is enclosed or designed and used to capture or suppress airborne asbestos. High-pressure water spray and compressed air must never be used on asbestos.

Does my workplace need an asbestos register?

Yes, unless the building was constructed after 31 December 1989, no asbestos has been identified and none is likely to be present. Registers must be reviewed at least every five years.

How big is 10 square metres of asbestos?

WorkSafe Queensland's Code of Practice describes it as approximately the size of a small bathroom.
