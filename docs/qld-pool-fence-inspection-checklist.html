QLD pool fence inspection checklist: what the inspector checks before a pool safety certificate

Last reviewed 11 November 2026 · Sources linked below · Written by Jay at Andwat Co, an Australian boilermaker/fabricator (Cert III Engineering, 12+ years in the trade). About

The Queensland pool barrier rules an inspector measures (1200 mm height, 100 mm gaps, the 900 mm non-climbable zone, self-closing gates, 1500 mm latches, CPR sign), when you need a pool safety certificate, and what happens after a Form 26. QBCC sources linked.

Most Queensland pools that fail a pool safety inspection fail on small things: a gate that swings shut but doesn't latch, a pot plant or tap inside the non-climbable zone, a gap under the fence where the ground has washed out. Each one means a non-conformity notice, a repair and a second visit, often in the middle of a sale or a new lease. This guide sets out the barrier rules a QBCC-licensed pool safety inspector measures against, so you can walk the fence with a tape measure first. It is general information, not a pool safety inspection or legal advice, and it covers Queensland only.

The short answer

  • Fence height: at least 1200 mm from finished ground level to the top, at every point along the fence.
  • Gaps: no more than 100 mm under the barrier and between vertical members (palings, rods, wires).
  • Non-climbable zone: a 900 mm zone with no foot or hand holds; climbable objects at least 900 mm away outside and 300 mm inside.
  • Gates: open away from the pool, self-close and self-latch from every open position, outside latch release at least 1500 mm above the ground.
  • CPR sign: at least 300 × 300 mm, durable and weatherproof, easy to see near the pool.
  • Certificate: needed to sell, buy or lease; valid 2 years (non-shared pool) or 1 year (shared pool).

When you need a pool safety certificate

Queensland's pool safety laws sit in the Building Act 1975, and the technical standard for barriers is Queensland Development Code MP 3.4 Swimming pool barriers, which calls up AS 1926.1-2012. Every regulated pool and outdoor spa must also be registered on the QBCC pool safety register. Registration is free, but not registering is an offence.

QBCC says you need a pool safety certificate if you are selling, buying or leasing a property with a pool. Only a pool safety inspector licensed by QBCC can issue one, and it confirms the pool complied at the time it was inspected. It is valid for 2 years for a non-shared pool (a house) and 1 year for a shared pool (units, body corporate). Owners of shared pools must display the current certificate near the main entrance or at a gate or door to the pool. A recent building certificate (Form 17 final inspection or Form 11 certificate of occupancy) lodged with QBCC can stand in for a certificate after a new pool or fence is built.

If a property is sold without a certificate, the seller gives a Form 36 notice of no pool safety certificate and the buyer must get a certificate within 90 days of settlement. A non-conformity notice doesn't extend that deadline.

The barrier checks, with the numbers

These are the figures from QBCC's pool safety guide and the Queensland Government guideline for pool owners and property agents. Measure them yourself before you book an inspection.

  1. Height: at least 1200 mm from finished ground level to the top of the barrier. On a slope or steps measure at right angles to the ground under the fence, and check the lowest point, not the average. No step, sill, deck or garden edge outside may reduce that 1200 mm.
  2. Gap under the fence: no more than 100 mm from finished ground level to the bottom of the barrier. Erosion, settled pavers and mulch that has been raked away are common causes of a fail.
  3. Gaps between verticals: no more than 100 mm. If there are horizontal rails inside the 900 mm non-climbable zone, gaps between verticals must be no more than 10 mm and the rails need a 60-degree fillet so they can't be stood on.
  4. Rails: on the outside, at least one gap of 900 mm or more between rails, measured from the top of one rail to the top of the rail below. If the outside rails are closer than 900 mm, fit 60-degree wedges and close the palings to under 10 mm, or build the fence at least 1800 mm high with the non-climbable zone on the inside.
  5. Non-climbable zone: a continuous 900 mm zone around the whole barrier, extending in an arc up and down from the top. For fences under 1800 mm it is on the outside; for 1800 mm and over it can be on either side, which is how most boundary fences comply. It also extends 900 mm past the end of a fence and where a fence meets another barrier or object.
  6. Climbable objects: anything with a horizontal surface over 10 mm that gives a young child a foot or hand hold must be at least 900 mm away on the outside and 300 mm on the inside. The guideline names climbable trees, outdoor furniture, BBQs, taps, pot plants, lattice, trellis and retaining walls. Smooth tree trunks and non-climbable plants are allowed.
  7. Stability: the fence must be on stable, solid ground, with no loose panels, rusted posts or broken welds on steel or aluminium sections.
  8. Windows and doors: a window or door that opens onto the pool area and forms part of the barrier must be protected, for example with a fixed security screen or by fixing it so it can't open more than 100 mm.
  9. Above-ground pools and spas: non-climbable sides 1200 mm high can form part of the barrier, with a compliant barrier and gate at the access point. Spas with lockable lids still need a compliant barrier.

Gates, latches and hinges

QBCC's gate rules are where a lot of otherwise good fences fail, because hinges and closers wear and nobody notices until the inspector opens the gate a few centimetres and lets go.

  • The gate must not open towards the pool.
  • It must self-close and self-latch from every open position, including just ajar. Never prop or tie it open.
  • The outside latch release must be at least 1500 mm above finished ground level (to the underside of the release) and 1400 mm above the top of the highest lower horizontal member.
  • An inside latch must be at least 150 mm below the top of the gate (or any hand hole) and covered by a shield with a 450 mm radius and no gaps over 10 mm.
  • Hinges must be at least 900 mm apart (top of lower hinge to top of upper hinge), or the lower hinge needs a 60-degree safety cap.

CPR sign

Every regulated pool needs a CPR sign attached to the barrier or displayed near the pool so anyone at the pool can see it. QBCC says it must be at least 300 mm × 300 mm, made of durable and weatherproof material, show how to perform CPR in line with ANZCOR Guideline 8 (30 chest compressions followed by 2 breaths), and include a clear statement of what to do in an emergency, such as calling 000. A faded or sun-cracked sign is an easy fail, so replace it before the inspection.

If the inspector issues a Form 26

If the inspector isn't satisfied the pool complies, they issue a Form 26 non-conformity notice that lists what doesn't comply and what to fix. They don't issue one if they re-inspect within 2 days and the pool now complies, or if you agree they will do minor repairs within 20 business days. After a Form 26:

  • You must have the same inspector re-inspect within 3 months. If you don't, the inspector must tell your council, which can fine you.
  • You can't get another inspector to issue a certificate within those 3 months.
  • You can appeal the notice to the Development Tribunal within 20 days.

Some repairs don't need building approval but must still meet the standard: repairing or adjusting up to 2.4 m of fence or 2 posts, adding a shield for the non-climbable zone, raising the fence height, laying paving or concrete to close the gap underneath, fixing a gate so it self-closes and self-latches, and screening or fixing a window or door. If you pay someone for work worth $3,300 or more (labour and materials) they need a QBCC contractor's licence, and owner work over $11,000 needs an owner-builder permit. Replacing the whole barrier means engaging a pool safety inspector before you start, with temporary fencing while the old fence is down.

A practical pre-inspection routine

  1. Check the property on the QBCC pool register: is the pool registered, and is there a current certificate?
  2. Walk the whole barrier with a tape measure, recording height, gap underneath and gaps between verticals at the worst points.
  3. Stand outside the fence and look for anything climbable within 900 mm: plants, furniture, taps, BBQs, the neighbour's side of a boundary fence.
  4. Open the gate from fully open, half open and just ajar, and let go each time. It must close and latch every time.
  5. Measure the latch height, check hinge spacing, and check the CPR sign.
  6. Fix what you find, take dated photos, and book the inspector once everything passes.

Keep a simple log after the certificate is issued as well: storms move fences, plants grow into the zone, and gate closers wear. A quick check each quarter and after big weather means the next certificate (or the next lease) isn't a surprise. Common questions

How high does a pool fence have to be in Queensland?

At least 1200 mm, measured from finished ground level on the outside to the top of the barrier, at every point along the fence. On sloping or stepped ground measure at right angles to the ground under the fence. The gap under the barrier and the gaps between vertical members can be no more than 100 mm.

When do I need a pool safety certificate in Queensland?

When you sell, buy or lease a property with a regulated pool. Only a QBCC-licensed pool safety inspector can issue one. It lasts 2 years for a non-shared pool and 1 year for a shared pool. If a property is sold without one, the buyer must get a certificate within 90 days of settlement.

What is the non-climbable zone?

A 900 mm zone around the barrier where a young child cannot get a hand or foot hold. Climbable objects such as BBQs, pot plants, taps, retaining walls or furniture must be at least 900 mm away from the barrier on the outside and 300 mm on the inside. For fences 1800 mm or higher the zone can be on the inside, measured down from the top.

What happens if my pool fails inspection?

The inspector issues a Form 26 non-conformity notice listing what to fix, unless they re-inspect within 2 days or agree to do minor repairs within 20 business days. You must have the same inspector re-inspect within 3 months, and you can't get a certificate from another inspector in that time. If you don't, the inspector must tell your council, which can fine you.
